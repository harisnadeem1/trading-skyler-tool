# Anatomy of the Ronin Flat Base

## Why this matters

A flat base is a strong stock resting near its highs. The base must tighten, hold support, and show less selling before the breakout is ready.

## Clear explanation

Do not call every sideways range a flat base. A Ronin flat base is a high, controlled, low-supply pause in a proven leader.

A flat base is a continuation structure formed when a strong stock pauses near its highs and absorbs supply without suffering meaningful technical damage.

It is not simply a horizontal rectangle.

### Requirement 1: Prior strength

The base should follow a meaningful expansion. The stock must already have demonstrated demand through a breakout, earnings gap, high-volume event, or sustained advance.

A sideways range after months of weakness is not the same setup.

### Requirement 2: High location

The best bases form:

- near recent or all-time highs
- above rising moving averages
- above the midpoint of the prior advance
- with limited overhead resistance
- inside a strong weekly trend

Holding high tells you that shareholders are not eager to sell.

### Requirement 3: Controlled depth

A shallow base is generally more constructive than a deep one because the stock retains more of its prior gain. The exact percentage varies with volatility, but the structure should feel controlled relative to the expansion that preceded it.

### Requirement 4: Tightness

The final section of the base should show:

- narrowing ranges
- tight closes
- inside days
- stable lows
- fewer wide red candles
- reduced volume
- a clearly visible pivot

The base can begin somewhat loose, but it should become tighter before it is actionable.

### Requirement 5: Support

Support may come from:

- the 8 or 21 EMA
- a prior breakout level
- the upper part of an earnings gap
- a daily higher low
- the base low

The stock should repeatedly defend the area without requiring dramatic recoveries.

### The squat subtype

A squat is a short, tight pause near highs, often while the 8 EMA catches up. It is attractive because the stock corrects through time, not through a deep decline.

### What a quality base communicates

The prior move attracted demand. The base tests whether supply can push price down. Tight action and dry volume show that supply is limited.

The pivot marks the point where immediate resistance may be cleared.

### How to apply it in real time

The geometry of a flat base is useful because it makes the behavior of supply visible. The stock has already advanced, so early buyers have profits and late buyers may be impatient. If those holders can sell without pushing price down materially, demand is absorbing the supply.

The base is therefore a test of ownership quality.

Location is the first filter. A high base near recent highs shows that the market accepts the new price area. A deep base far below the high may still recover, but it is no longer the same continuation structure.

Depth must be judged relative to the stock’s volatility and the prior advance; the goal is controlled correction, not a fixed percentage.

The final third of the base receives the greatest attention. Early candles describe the initial reaction; late candles reveal whether the stock has actually become ready. Tight closes, inside days, stable lows, and a rising 8 or 21 EMA show that the range is becoming organized.

A base that stays wide until the breakout attempt has not completed the contraction process.

## Key rules / steps

1. Confirm a meaningful prior expansion and real leadership.
2. Require the base to form high in the larger weekly and daily range.
3. Compare base depth with the stock’s normal volatility and prior move.
4. Inspect the final third for tighter ranges, stable closes, and reduced supply.
5. Mark the lower structural boundary and the upper pivot before price breaks out.
6. Reject ranges that repeatedly violate both sides or cut through major averages.

**Rule to remember**

> **A rectangle is not enough. Prior strength, high location, contraction, and a clear pivot must all be present.**

## Common mistakes

- Drawing a box around any sideways action and calling it a base
- Ignoring a deep retracement because the eventual pivot looks clean
- Focusing on the first days of the base while the final days remain loose
- Assuming a base becomes better simply because it lasts longer

### Coach's note

Watch for **anticipation and breakout FOMO**. A clear pivot can make you enter early or chase late. Emotional neutrality means waiting for the rule and accepting that some moves leave without you.

Your job is to see readiness developing, not merely to identify a box.
