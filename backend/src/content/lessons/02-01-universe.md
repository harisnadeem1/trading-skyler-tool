# Define the Trading Universe

## Why this matters

You do not need to scan every security. Start with liquid US stocks that move enough to justify a swing trade and can be entered without excessive slippage.

## Clear explanation

Your scanner gives you candidates, not trades. Your job is to reduce the list to genuine leaders with clean structure, enough liquidity, enough movement, and a real reason for institutions to care.

The Ronin system focuses on liquid US-listed stocks capable of producing meaningful multi-day or multi-week movement. The objective is not to find the cheapest stock. It is to find a vehicle that can attract institutional demand and can be entered and exited without unnecessary friction.

### Basic universe filters

A practical starting universe normally includes:

- common stocks listed on major US exchanges
- price generally above $10
- adequate average daily dollar volume
- sufficient average daily range or ATR
- no persistent liquidity gaps
- reliable price and volume history

These are not magic numbers. They are filters that remove vehicles poorly suited to the strategy.

### Dollar volume matters more than share volume

One million shares in a $3 stock is very different from one million shares in a $200 stock. Average dollar volume gives a more realistic picture of liquidity.

For a swing trader, liquidity helps with:

- reducing slippage
- entering around pivots
- trimming without disturbing price
- avoiding exaggerated spreads
- interpreting volume more reliably

### Movement must justify attention

A stock can be liquid but too slow. The system prefers stocks with enough ADR or ATR to produce asymmetric opportunities. Movement should not come from random illiquidity; it should occur in a liquid stock with organized demand.

### Exclusions

Remove or deprioritize:

- ETFs when scanning for individual growth leaders
- very low-priced stocks
- thinly traded names
- stocks with repeated gaps caused by poor liquidity
- charts with unreliable volume
- stocks that cannot support your required position size cleanly

### Quality before quantity

A scanner that returns hundreds of names has not completed the work. It has created raw material. The real edge comes from manually reviewing charts and building a focused list.

The goal is not to monitor every possibility. It is to know the best candidates deeply enough that you can recognize when their structures become actionable.

### How to apply it in real time

The trading universe should remove instruments that are too illiquid, too slow, too damaged, or structurally inappropriate for the system. The objective is not to find the greatest number of candidates. It is to create a pool where a valid pattern has enough movement and institutional participation to matter.

Ronin Charts focuses on US-listed common stocks rather than ETFs as setup candidates. A practical starting filter can include price above $10, adequate average dollar volume, meaningful average daily range, and a preference for companies with sufficient institutional ownership and market capitalization. These are starting points, not laws.

A stock can pass every numerical filter and still be rejected because the chart is loose, the group is weak, or the catalyst is absent.

Liquidity must be evaluated in dollar terms as well as share volume. One million shares of a $5 stock is not equivalent to one million shares of a $200 stock. The position should be executable without your order becoming the market.

## Real example or short trading story

A scanner returns two stocks. One trades millions of shares but has a low price and poor dollar liquidity. The other has strong average dollar volume, enough ADR%, and a clean weekly trend.

Share volume alone makes the first stock look liquid. Dollar volume reveals the difference.

You choose the stock that can be entered, managed, and exited without the trade mechanics becoming the main risk.

## Key rules / steps

1. Exclude ETFs and instruments that do not match the stock-selection process.
2. Use a minimum price and average dollar-volume threshold appropriate to account size.
3. Prefer stocks with enough ADR to create meaningful R multiples without requiring months.
4. Check spreads and slippage, not just historical volume.
5. Keep numerical filters broad enough to find emerging leaders but strict enough to remove obvious noise.

**Rule to remember**

> **A stock must be tradable before its pattern can matter.**

## Common mistakes

- Keeping too many mediocre names because the scanner returned them
- Confusing a scanner result with a qualified stock
- Using market capitalization alone as a substitute for liquidity
- Ignoring spreads in lower-priced stocks
- Lowering liquidity requirements because a pattern looks exciting

### Coach's note

Watch for **focus and novelty bias**. Random movers and social-media excitement pull attention away from prepared leaders. A focused list reduces emotional decisions.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Run the same setup scan with three liquidity thresholds. Review the stocks removed at each step and note whether any genuine leaders were lost. Choose the lowest threshold that still gives clean execution for your account, then document it as a configurable rule rather than a universal truth.

### Reflection

1. Which candidate would you remove first, and what evidence makes it weaker?
2. How could FOMO or information noise pull you away from the prepared watchlist?
3. What facts must be recorded before a stock earns a place on your focus list?
