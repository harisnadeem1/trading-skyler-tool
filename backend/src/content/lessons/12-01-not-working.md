# When the Trade Is Not Working

## Why this matters

A trade that immediately loses the pivot, returns into the base, or breaks the support that justified the entry is not working.

## Clear explanation

Once you are in the trade, manage the chart—not your fear of losing profit and not your need to be right.

A trade that fails quickly provides useful information. Your job is to recognize when the original thesis is no longer valid rather than negotiate with the chart.

### Flat-base failure

Warnings:

- price falls back into the base
- pivot is lost on volume
- breakout-day low breaks
- the stock closes weakly
- RS deteriorates
- the market rejects similar breakouts

### Pullback failure

Warnings:

- reclaimed support is lost again
- the higher low fails
- selling volume expands
- price closes below the pullback low
- group peers break support

### Time stop

A trade may not hit the stop but still fail to make progress. If a breakout remains trapped around the pivot while the market and group advance, demand may be insufficient. Time-based decisions should be defined in the playbook rather than invented during frustration.

### Do not average down automatically

Adding to a losing position because price is cheaper increases exposure while evidence is worsening. Adds belong in a separate rule set and generally require new confirmation.

### Small loss mindset

A planned loss is a business expense. The damage comes from turning a small invalidation into a large, emotional position.

### How to apply it in real time

A trade that is not working usually stops behaving like the setup that justified entry. A flat-base breakout may fail to hold the pivot, lose the breakout-day low, or reverse on expanding volume. A controlled pullback may fail the reclaim, break the higher low, or accept below the key support area.

The speed of failure matters. Immediate failure after a strong-looking trigger suggests the demand was not real. Slow, low-volume drift may justify patience if structure remains intact, but repeated weak closes and inability to advance are information.

You should distinguish “not yet working” from “thesis invalid.”

The planned stop is the final line, not an invitation to ignore earlier evidence. Partial or full exit before the hard stop can be valid when the trigger clearly fails, but this must be defined and reviewed to avoid emotional micromanagement.

## Real example or short trading story

A flat-base breakout loses the pivot and then breaks the breakout-day low on expanding volume.

You feel the urge to wait because the stop has not yet produced the loss you hoped to avoid. But the trade is no longer behaving like the setup you bought.

You act on the invalidation. Loyalty belongs to the process, not to the ticker.

## Key rules / steps

1. Compare current behavior with the exact confirmation expected at entry.
2. Watch pivot acceptance, low-of-day behavior, and volume after breakout.
3. For pullbacks, monitor reclaim quality and the most recent higher low.
4. Identify repeated weak closes or inability to produce price progress.
5. Exit when structural invalidation occurs; do not negotiate with the stop.
6. Record whether any early discretionary exit followed a predefined rule.

**Rule to remember**

> **When the reason for the trade is gone, the trade is gone.**

## Common mistakes

- Managing the unrealized P&L instead of managing the chart
- Calling normal consolidation failure because profit did not appear immediately
- Ignoring immediate high-volume rejection
- Moving the stop lower to wait for the next support
- Exiting every small pullback out of fear after previous losses

### Coach's note

Watch for **greed, fear, and the disposition effect**. You may sell winners too early and hold failures too long. Structure must determine management, not the desire to feel safe or avoid regret.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Replay twenty trades from entry forward. Pause after every daily close and label them Working, Neutral, or Failing with one reason. Compare the label with the original stop and final outcome.

The purpose is to improve behavioral reading without turning every candle into a decision.

### Reflection

1. What chart behavior—not profit or fear—would change your management decision?
2. Which trailing or trimming method was chosen before the trade became emotional?
3. How will you distinguish a normal pullback from real structural failure?
