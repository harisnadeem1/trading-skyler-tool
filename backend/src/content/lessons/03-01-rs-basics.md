# RS Line, RS Rating, and Price Leadership

## Why this matters

Relative strength compares a stock with the market. The RS line shows ongoing comparison, while an RS Rating summarizes relative performance into a rank.

## Clear explanation

Relative strength tells you how a stock is behaving compared with the market. It is not RSI. Use it to confirm leadership, never to replace price and structure.

Relative strength compares a stock’s performance with a benchmark, usually SPY or another broad index. It answers a simple question: **Is this stock acting better or worse than the market?**

### RS line

An RS line is commonly calculated as stock price divided by benchmark price. A rising line means the stock is outperforming. A falling line means it is underperforming.

The line is most useful for direction and structure:

- Is it trending higher?
- Is it making higher highs and higher lows?
- Is it near a new high?
- Is it breaking out before price?
- Is it holding firm during a market decline?

### RS rating

An RS rating ranks a stock against a broad universe over a defined period. A rating of 90 means the stock has outperformed roughly 90% of the comparison universe over the rating period.

The exact provider and formula can differ. Treat the rating as a filter, not as a trade signal.

### Price leadership remains primary

A high rating cannot repair a damaged chart. Price must still show:

- constructive weekly and daily structure
- proximity to highs
- rising moving averages
- clean support
- a valid setup

### RS new high before price

One of the strongest clues occurs when the RS line reaches a new high while price remains below its pivot. The stock is quietly outperforming and may be absorbing supply better than the index.

This is especially useful inside a flat base. The price may appear inactive, but the RS line reveals hidden strength.

### RS divergence warnings

Be cautious when price approaches a new high but the RS line makes a lower high. This does not automatically invalidate the setup, but it suggests the stock is no longer leading with the same force.

### How to apply it in real time

Relative strength is the comparison between a stock and a benchmark, not the stock’s absolute price trend. An RS line usually divides the stock price by SPY or another benchmark. If the line rises, the stock is outperforming even if both are declining.

An RS rating compresses a longer performance comparison into a percentile-like score. Price leadership is the actual behavior visible in the chart.

These tools should agree, but they are not interchangeable. The RS rating helps filter the universe. The RS line shows when leadership is improving or deteriorating.

The price chart shows whether that leadership exists in a tradable structure. A stock with RS 99 can still be extended, choppy, or late-stage. A rising RS line inside a tight base may be more useful than the rating alone.

The system prefers elite relative strength, often RS 90 or higher for core candidates, but the threshold is a filter rather than a trigger.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock rises 2% on a day when QQQ rises 3%. Price is up, but the stock is not leading that session. Another stock stays flat while QQQ falls 2%; its relative-strength line improves.

This is why relative strength is about **performance versus the market**, not simply whether the candle is green.

You still read price first, then use the RS line and RS Rating to confirm the leadership story.

## Key rules / steps

1. Use the RS rating to narrow the universe to top performers.
2. Use the RS line to detect improvement, divergence, and new highs before price.
3. Confirm that price is also near highs and structurally healthy.
4. Compare against the benchmark most relevant to the stock, while keeping SPY/QQQ as broad references.
5. Review RS behavior during the actual base or pullback, not only over twelve months.

**Rule to remember**

> **RS confirms leadership; it does not replace price structure.**

## Common mistakes

- Treating an RS number as a buy signal without reading the chart
- Assuming RS means RSI
- Buying solely because the RS line reaches a new high
- Ignoring price extension or poor liquidity
- Using different benchmarks until one creates the desired conclusion

### Coach's note

Watch for **confirmation bias**. Once you like a stock, the brain searches for evidence that agrees. RS must be used as a filter, not as permission to ignore deterioration.
