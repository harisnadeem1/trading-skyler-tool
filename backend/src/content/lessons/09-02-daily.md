# The Daily Chart: Setup and Invalidation

## Why this matters

The daily chart defines the pattern, support, pivot, and invalidation. It is the main decision chart in the system.

## Clear explanation

The weekly chart gives strategic context. The daily chart defines the setup and invalidation. The 30-minute chart refines execution.

The daily chart is the operating chart of the Ronin system. It defines the setup, volume pattern, pivot, support, and structural invalidation.

### Daily responsibilities

- classify Flat Base or Controlled Pullback
- identify the prior expansion
- mark key levels
- evaluate volume contraction
- locate the 8, 21, 50, 200, and weekly 8 EMA
- assess RS
- define what confirmation should look like
- determine where the thesis is wrong

### Daily chart clarity

Use enough history to understand the prior move and major resistance, but zoom in enough to judge candle quality. Avoid overloading the chart with indicators.

### Setup statement

Write one sentence:

> “This is a first controlled pullback after an earnings gap, holding the prior breakout and rising 21 EMA on contracting volume.”

If the sentence requires vague language, the setup may not be clear.

### Invalidation statement

Write:

> “The setup loses validity if price closes below the pullback low and prior breakout on expanding volume.”

The invalidation should be structural, not emotional.

### Daily close versus intraday movement

A stock can temporarily move below support and recover. The importance of an intraday violation depends on the setup and trigger. Use the 30-minute chart to refine, but do not ignore decisive daily damage.

### How to apply it in real time

The daily chart is the central decision chart. It identifies the parent setup, the prior expansion, the contraction, key levels, volume sequence, moving-average relationship, and structural invalidation. If these elements are not clear on the daily chart, the 30-minute chart should not be used to manufacture clarity.

A valid daily plan is conditional. It describes what must happen before entry: “Flat base under 82.40; volume dry; enter only if price clears the pivot with improving run rate and acceptable LOD distance.” This is different from a prediction that the stock will break out.

The daily chart also determines what would change the classification. A controlled pullback can become deterioration after a high-volume support violation. A flat base can become chop after repeated wide reversals.

Updating the label is analysis, not inconsistency.

## Real example or short trading story

You write two sentences before the open: “The setup is a Controlled Pullback into the 21 EMA and prior breakout area. The thesis fails below the pullback low.”

Those sentences force clarity. If you cannot describe the setup and invalidation simply, the chart is probably not ready.

The daily chart is where the pattern and the structural failure point are defined.

## Key rules / steps

1. Label the stock as Flat Base, Controlled Pullback, or No Setup.
2. Mark the prior expansion and the catalyst that created attention.
3. Mark pivot, support zone, and structural invalidation.
4. Read the volume sequence through expansion and contraction.
5. Check the 8, 21, 50, 200, and weekly 8 EMA relationships only as relevant.
6. Write the exact confirmation required before using the 30-minute chart.

**Rule to remember**

> **Name the daily setup before opening the 30-minute chart.**

## Common mistakes

- Letting a 5-minute fluctuation overrule the weekly and daily structure
- Using intraday action to override an unclear daily chart
- Drawing too many levels and losing the main structure
- Keeping the original setup label after the daily chart changes
- Entering because the stock is near a moving average without a daily thesis

### Coach's note

Watch for **timeframe hopping**. When a trade becomes uncomfortable, traders often search another timeframe for reassurance. Each timeframe must keep its assigned job.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

For twenty watchlist names, write a three-sentence daily plan: setup and context, required trigger, and disqualifier. If any plan needs more than three sentences to explain the basic structure, simplify the chart or remove it from the actionable list.

### Reflection

2. Which timeframe is currently giving conflicting evidence?
3. Would the trade still qualify if you hid the 30-minute chart?
