# Anatomy of a Quality Earnings Gap

## Why this matters

A quality earnings gap can reset how institutions value a company. The event earns the stock a place on the watchlist; the later structure creates the trade.

## Clear explanation

An earnings gap, HVE, or HV1 event can reveal institutional urgency. It earns the stock your attention. It does not create an automatic entry.

An earnings gap can reprice a company in one session. It often marks the beginning of institutional accumulation, but not every gap creates a durable trend.

### Quality characteristics

A constructive earnings gap often includes:

- a meaningful gap above prior resistance
- very high volume
- a wide daily range
- a close in the upper part of the range
- strong relative performance
- a real beat, raise, or material change in expectations
- continued support over the following sessions

### Location matters

A gap from a long, constructive base is different from a gap directly into major overhead supply. Prefer gaps that clear a meaningful level and create open space above.

### Closing behavior

A gap that closes near the high shows demand remained present throughout the session. A large gap that reverses and closes near the low warns that supply overwhelmed the initial excitement.

### The gap creates a new map

Mark:

- gap-day high
- gap-day low
- midpoint of the gap-day range
- prior resistance
- first consolidation after the gap

These levels often become future support or resistance.

### Do not chase automatically

The event day proves urgency, but the next controlled structure often offers a better opportunity. The system prefers the first pullback or a tight post-gap base rather than buying a vertical move with no nearby support.

### Follow-through test

Over the next several sessions, ask:

- Does price hold most of the gap?
- Does volume contract on pullbacks?
- Does RS remain strong?
- Does the stock form higher lows?
- Do institutions defend the gap support area?

The market’s response after the news matters more than the headline itself.

### How to apply it in real time

A quality earnings gap is more than a large opening percentage. It represents a potential revaluation: new information causes institutions to accept a materially different price. The most useful gaps break meaningful resistance, trade extraordinary dollar volume, and close in the upper portion of the range.

The gap’s location matters. A gap from a long, tight base into new highs is structurally different from a gap that runs directly into years of overhead supply. The close matters too.

A large gap that gives back most of the move may show supply overwhelming the initial excitement.

The event day creates levels that can remain important for weeks: the gap-day high, low, midpoint, and pre-gap resistance. These levels help judge later digestion and first pullback opportunities. The system does not require buying the event day.

Often the better trade develops after the initial urgency is absorbed.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock gaps 18% after earnings. It opens strong, but by the close it has reversed and finished near the low of the day.

The size of the gap looked impressive. The closing behavior tells a different story.

You map the gap high, low, and prior structure, then wait to see whether the market accepts the new price area before treating the stock as a continuation candidate.

## Key rules / steps

1. Confirm that the report or guidance represents a meaningful catalyst.
2. Map the pre-gap base and overhead resistance.
3. Compare event-day volume with historical volume and dollar volume.
4. Judge the gap close, intraday range, and ability to hold the opening move.
5. Mark the gap-day high, low, midpoint, and prior pivot for future reference.
6. Wait for a Flat Base or Controlled Pullback rather than chasing automatically.

**Rule to remember**

> **Do not confuse the catalyst day with an automatic entry.**

## Common mistakes

- Chasing the event day instead of waiting for the first clean structure
- Calling any gap a quality earnings gap
- Ignoring a weak close because the opening move was large
- Buying into major overhead supply
- Forgetting that the gap is context, not a third parent setup

### Coach's note

Watch for **FOMO after expansion**. Large gaps and high-volume candles create urgency. The event proves demand, but urgency does not guarantee an efficient entry.
