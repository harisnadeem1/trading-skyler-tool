# Flat Base Playbook

## Why this matters

This playbook combines every flat-base rule into one qualification and execution checklist.

## Clear explanation

This module connects the entire system into one operating process. You should be able to explain every decision before you take it.

Use this playbook only after the stock passes the market and leadership filters.

### Qualification

- supportive or at least Constructive market
- liquid US stock
- strong RS and group leadership
- meaningful prior expansion or catalyst
- price near highs
- rising weekly and daily moving averages
- shallow, high base
- final volatility contraction
- declining volume
- clear pivot
- limited overhead resistance

### Trigger

- breakout above the final pivot
- relative volume improving
- strong close or intraday acceptance
- 30-minute pivot, higher low, or opening-range compression
- LOD ATR preferably below 60%
- entry-to-stop distance generally no greater than one ATR

### Rejection

- loose range
- repeated distribution
- unclear pivot
- late-stage weekly structure
- weak RS
- breakout far above support
- hostile market regime

### Management

- exit if price loses the pivot and structural stop
- remain while higher lows and support hold
- trim only for extension, concentration, or planned reasons
- trail according to the speed of the trend

### Post-trade review

Compare the trade with the original base screenshot. Did the final contraction exist? Was volume truly dry?

Did the breakout attract demand? Was the entry efficient?

### How to apply it in real time

The Flat Base Playbook integrates the entire system. The stock must be a liquid leader with a real catalyst or institutional engine. The market must support long swing trades.

The weekly chart must show constructive trend and acceptable overhead supply. The daily chart must show prior expansion, high location, controlled depth, final contraction, dry volume, and a clear pivot.

Only after qualification does the 30-minute chart matter. The trigger can be a compact pivot, higher low, or opening compression through the daily level. Volume must show enough participation, and the entry must remain efficient relative to low of day and the structural stop.

Management begins with pivot acceptance. Immediate return into the base, heavy rejection, or loss of the breakout-day low warns that the trade is failing. A successful breakout should separate, then build new support that can guide the trail or future add.

## Real example or short trading story

You start with the market regime, then move to a proven leader with prior expansion. The daily chart shows a high, shallow base, final contraction, a clear pivot, and dry volume.

On the 30-minute chart, price forms a qualified trigger while entry efficiency remains acceptable.

Every part of the Flat Base Playbook connects. If one critical gate is missing, the trade is not complete.

## Key rules / steps

1. Market regime is Aggressive or Constructive enough for the setup.
2. Stock is a genuine leader with liquidity, RS, catalyst, and group support.
3. Weekly trend is constructive and not directly under major supply.
4. Daily base is high, shallow, tight, dry, and supported by rising structure.
5. Pivot is preplanned and breakout shows confirmation.
6. Entry efficiency, stop, size, and portfolio heat satisfy rules.
7. Post-entry behavior accepts the pivot and creates follow-through.

**Rule to remember**

> **A playbook removes improvisation at the moment of action.**

## Common mistakes

- Using a checklist mechanically without understanding why each item exists
- Using the playbook as a checklist to rationalize a favorite stock
- Ignoring one critical failure because most boxes are checked
- Entering before the final contraction or after the breakout becomes extended
- Confusing a broad late-stage range with a flat base

### Coach's note

Watch for **consistency under pressure**. Mastery means the same decision sequence is used when excited, afraid, bored, winning, or losing.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Use the full playbook on twenty historical bases. Score each category 0, 1, or 2 and hide the outcome. Define a minimum quality threshold before revealing results.

Review false positives and false negatives, but do not change the threshold until the full sample is complete.

### Reflection

1. Can you explain the full decision from market regime to post-trade review?
2. Which gate would reject this trade first?
3. What part of the playbook still requires deliberate practice before you use it live?
