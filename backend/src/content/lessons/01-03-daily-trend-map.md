# Build the Daily Trend Map

## Why this matters

The Trend Map is a short daily checklist that classifies the environment as Aggressive, Constructive, Defensive, or No Trade.

## Clear explanation

Before you judge any stock, decide what the market is rewarding. The same setup can work in one regime and fail quickly in another.

The Trend Map turns market observations into a consistent daily decision. It should be updated after the close and checked again during the first 10, 20, and 30 minutes of the next session.

### End-of-day inputs

Review the following:

### Index structure

- SPY and QQQ relative to the 8, 21, and 50-day averages
- slope of the 21 and 50-day averages
- recent higher highs and higher lows
- distribution days and failed recovery attempts
- distance from support and resistance

### Leadership

- number of liquid leaders near highs
- breakout success rate over the last one to two weeks
- quality of controlled pullbacks
- RS behavior
- industry-group concentration

### Breadth and participation

Use breadth as supporting evidence, not as a standalone signal. Ask whether gains are broad or dependent on a small number of large stocks.

### Portfolio feedback

Record whether open positions are:

- expanding
- stalling
- violating pivots
- breaking support
- becoming extended

### Score the evidence

A simple score prevents emotional labels. Award points for constructive evidence and subtract points for deterioration. For example:

- index above rising 21-day average: +1
- index above rising 50-day average: +1
- multiple leaders at new highs: +1
- recent breakouts holding: +1
- repeated failed breakouts: −1
- leaders losing the 50-day average: −1
- heavy distribution: −1

Map the total to a regime, but keep the written explanation. The number is a summary, not the reason.

### Opening refresh

The first 30 minutes can confirm or challenge the overnight plan.

At 10 minutes, observe:

- gap behavior
- whether leaders hold their opening lows
- whether the index is accepting or rejecting overnight levels

At 20 minutes, observe:

- opening-range direction
- relative volume
- whether watchlist names are forming tight pivots or reversing

At 30 minutes, decide whether the session is acting better, worse, or as expected. Do not completely rewrite the higher-timeframe regime because of one opening move, but adjust tactical aggression.

### Daily output

Your Trend Map should produce:

- current regime
- confidence level
- preferred exposure range
- setup priority
- specific conditions that would improve or weaken the view

Example:

> **Constructive, moderate confidence.** QQQ remains above a rising 50-day average, but leadership is narrow. Prioritize first pullbacks in earnings leaders. Avoid marginal breakouts unless volume expands. Upgrade if recent breakouts hold; downgrade if leaders lose the 21-day average together.

### How to apply it in real time

The Trend Map converts broad market information into an operating decision. It should be simple enough to update every day and structured enough to prevent mood from becoming analysis. The map is completed after the close for the next session, then refreshed after the open when early evidence becomes available.

The map should include index structure, breadth, leadership, breakout quality, and the behavior of your own positions. No single component controls the final state. A strong close in QQQ can be outweighed by collapsing breadth and multiple leader failures.

Likewise, a weak index day may remain Constructive when leading stocks hold support and selling volume contracts.

The output is not a prediction such as “the market will rise tomorrow.” The output is a regime, an exposure posture, and a short list of conditions that would improve or weaken the classification.

## Real example or short trading story

At the close, the market looks Constructive: indexes hold support, leadership is narrow, and recent breakouts are mixed. The next morning, the first 30 minutes bring heavy gap reversals and weakness in your leader basket.

You do not throw away the end-of-day map. You refresh it with the new opening evidence.

The map is a working decision tool, not a permanent forecast.

## Key rules / steps

1. After the close: mark SPY and QQQ relative to the 8, 21, 50, and 200-day averages.
2. Review breadth and the number of leaders near highs versus leaders breaking support.
3. Score recent breakout and pullback follow-through.
4. Classify the regime and write the evidence in one sentence.
5. Before the open: note gaps, major news, and whether the planned posture needs adjustment.
6. After 10, 20, and 30 minutes: update only if opening behavior materially changes the evidence.

**Rule to remember**

> **Classify first; trade second.**

## Common mistakes

- Using one index candle to declare the whole market healthy or broken
- Building a dashboard with so many indicators that the conclusion is always ambiguous
- Changing the regime because of one opening candle
- Using the Trend Map as an automatic buy/sell signal
- Failing to record why the classification changed

### Coach's note

Watch for **patience and activity bias**. A quiet market can create the false feeling that you are falling behind. Activity is not productivity; the regime decides how much opportunity exists.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Create a one-page Trend Map template with five sections: indexes, breadth, leaders, setup follow-through, and current positions. Backfill it for ten sessions without changing the fields. At the end, remove any field that never changed a decision and add no new field unless it clearly would have.

Create a Trend Map for twenty historical sessions without looking at the following day. Then review what actually happened. The objective is not perfect prediction; it is consistent preparation.

### Reflection

1. What market evidence would make you change your regime classification?
2. How would impatience or the urge to trade distort this decision?
3. What must your leader basket show before you become more aggressive?
