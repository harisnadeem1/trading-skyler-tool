# Controlled Pullback Playbook

## Why this matters

This playbook combines trend, support, contraction, confirmation, and failure rules for controlled pullbacks.

## Clear explanation

This module connects the entire system into one operating process. You should be able to explain every decision before you take it.

### Qualification

- proven leader with recent expansion
- strong weekly trend
- first or high-quality second pullback
- lower volume during decline
- smaller ranges
- key level aligned with 8, 21, or weekly 8 EMA
- RS remains constructive
- no major distribution

### Support map

Mark:

- prior breakout
- earnings-gap high, low, and midpoint
- HVE level
- daily higher low
- relevant moving averages

### Trigger

- higher low
- reclaim
- reversal candle
- inside-day break
- 30-minute pivot
- improving relative volume
- efficient distance from LOD and stop

### Rejection

- vertical decline
- expanding sell volume
- multiple broken levels
- repeated failed reclaims
- RS collapse
- mature trend with widening weekly ranges
- no clear return of demand

### Management

- exit if support and structural low fail
- remain while trend and higher lows hold
- use 8, 21, or weekly structure based on trade speed
- record whether the pullback subtype matched the plan

### How to apply it in real time

The Controlled Pullback Playbook begins with a proven trend, not with price touching a moving average. The stock should have a meaningful catalyst, strong RS, and a clear prior expansion. The retreat should show reduced supply, limited damage, and approach a preplanned support area.

First pullbacks after earnings gaps receive priority because the trend may be early. Second pullbacks can qualify when the weekly 8 EMA and a key horizontal level align, but later-stage conditions demand stronger evidence. In every case, the stock must confirm that buyers are returning through a reclaim, higher low, reversal candle, or 30-minute pivot.

The playbook rejects vertical selling, expanding volume, repeated support failure, weak RS, and mechanical moving-average touches. It also rejects a good reaction when the entry becomes too far from low of day or the structural stop.

## Real example or short trading story

A leader pulls back toward the 21 EMA and a prior breakout area on declining volume. The weekly trend remains constructive, and the daily chart begins to stabilize.

You wait for the 30-minute confirmation, check Low-of-Day ATR and structural stop distance, then decide whether the trade still fits.

The playbook prevents “buy the moving average” from replacing the full Controlled Pullback setup.

## Key rules / steps

1. Market environment supports long continuation trades.
2. Leader has a fresh or still-relevant institutional engine.
3. Pullback is controlled in range, volume, and depth.
4. Support combines meaningful price structure with a relevant moving average.
5. Price shows confirmation rather than only touching the level.
6. Entry efficiency and risk mathematics remain acceptable.
7. Post-entry structure creates higher lows or renewed expansion.

**Rule to remember**

> **Buy the return of strength near support, not weakness by itself.**

## Common mistakes

- Using a checklist mechanically without understanding why each item exists
- Buying weakness before confirmation
- Treating every pullback number as equivalent regardless of trend maturity
- Ignoring the market because the stock held support once
- Keeping the “controlled” label after ranges and volume expand

### Coach's note

Watch for **consistency under pressure**. Mastery means the same decision sequence is used when excited, afraid, bored, winning, or losing.
