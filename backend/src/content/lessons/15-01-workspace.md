# Build the Ronin TradingView Workspace

## Why this matters

Create a clean chart where price, structure, volume, relative strength, and entry efficiency each have one clear job.

## Clear explanation

Your TradingView workspace should make the Ronin process easier to see. Indicators support the decision; they do not make the decision.

A chart should help you make a decision quickly. It should not force you to decode ten indicators before you can see price. The Ronin workspace is built around a simple hierarchy:

1. **Price tells you what is happening.**
2. **Moving averages show trend and areas of interest.**
3. **Volume shows participation and supply.**
4. **Relative strength shows leadership.**
5. **Swing data measures movement, liquidity, and entry efficiency.**

> **Indicators organize evidence. They do not replace the setup.**

### The three-chart workflow

### Weekly chart — strategic context

Use the weekly chart to answer:

- Is the stock in a healthy long-term uptrend?
- Is it near highs or trapped below major overhead supply?
- Is the weekly 8 EMA rising and supporting price?
- Is the current move early, developing, or already mature?

The weekly chart is not used for a precise entry. It decides whether the daily setup deserves attention.

### Daily chart — setup and structure

The daily chart is the main Ronin chart. Use it to identify:

- prior expansion
- the Flat Base Breakout or Controlled Pullback
- key horizontal levels
- volume contraction
- the 8 EMA, 21 EMA, 50 EMA, weekly 8 EMA, and 200 SMA
- the pivot, support, and invalidation area
- relative-strength behavior

### 30-minute chart — execution

Open the 30-minute chart only after the daily setup qualifies. Use it for:

- a higher low
- a reclaim
- a tight pivot
- opening-range compression
- Low-of-Day ATR distance
- live relative or projected volume

A clean 30-minute chart cannot repair a weak daily setup.

### Keep the workspace visually quiet

Turn off anything that does not answer a specific question. The default Ronin layout should contain:

- candlesticks
- 8 EMA
- 21 EMA
- 50 EMA
- 200 SMA
- weekly 8 EMA displayed on the daily chart
- MarketSmith Volumes
- RS line or RS Rating display
- Swing Data table
- ATR multiple from the 50-MA

Do not add oscillators because they look sophisticated. If a tool does not change a decision, remove it.

### Build it in this order

1. Open a liquid US stock on the **1D** timeframe.
2. Apply the Ronin Full palette from the next lessons.
3. Add the Moving Averages For All Timeframes indicator.
4. Add MarketSmith Volumes in the lower panel.
5. Add Swing Data in a compact corner table.
6. Add ATR% multiple from 50-MA as a small extension readout.
7. Add the RS tool already used in your Ronin layout, if it is separate from Swing Data.
8. Save the indicator template as **Ronin Full**.
9. Save separate layouts for **Weekly / Daily** and **30-Minute Execution** if screen space is limited.

### Final test

Load a chart with a clean uptrend, a flat base, a controlled pullback, and a failed breakout. You should be able to identify the trend and pattern before reading any indicator value. If the indicators dominate your attention, reduce their line width, size, or visibility.

## Real example or short trading story

You open a chart with twelve indicators, multiple bright colors, and labels covering price. The setup is there, but the workspace makes it harder to see.

You rebuild in order: clean canvas, candles, moving averages, volume, RS, Swing Data, and the extension tool.

Every element must answer a Ronin question. If it does not influence the decision, it does not deserve screen space.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Start with a clean chart.** Remove tools that do not influence a Ronin decision.
2. **Add price and moving averages first.** Keep the daily and weekly 8 EMA visually separate.
3. **Add volume, RS, and Swing Data.** Use them for participation, leadership, liquidity, and entry efficiency.
4. **Add the extension tool last.** Keep it small and contextual.
5. **Save the complete layout as Ronin Full.**

**Rule to remember**

> The chart is a decision surface. Every color and every tool must earn its place.

## Common mistakes

- Adding tools that do not change a decision
- Using so many colors that price structure becomes secondary
- Letting the workspace become a substitute for chart-reading skill

### Coach's note

Watch for **tool dependency**. A clean workspace improves attention, but no indicator can replace market context, price structure, or independent judgment.
