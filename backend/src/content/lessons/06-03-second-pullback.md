# Second Pullback and Weekly 8 EMA Rejection

## Why this matters

A second pullback can work, but it needs more proof because the trend is older. Weekly 8 EMA support and a major key level can create that proof.

## Clear explanation

A moving-average touch is not a buy signal. **You must judge the reaction, not the comfort of the touch.** A controlled pullback needs a proven trend, an orderly retreat, meaningful support, and proof that demand has returned.

A second pullback can offer another continuation entry, but the stock is further into its trend. The setup must meet a higher standard.

### Required context

Prefer cases where:

- the first pullback succeeded
- price produced another meaningful expansion
- RS remains high
- the weekly trend is intact
- the stock returns to the rising weekly 8 EMA
- a major horizontal key level aligns with the average
- volume contracts
- price rejects support with strength

### Rejection evidence

Look for:

- an undercut and reclaim
- a strong weekly or daily close
- a higher low
- a 30-minute reversal pivot
- improving RS
- volume increasing on the bounce

### Maturity warning

Every additional pullback increases the chance that the trend is becoming mature. Be cautious when:

- weekly ranges widen
- the stock repeatedly loses the 21-day average
- RS makes lower highs
- each bounce becomes weaker
- the stock is far above the 200-day average
- the group is losing leadership

### How to apply it in real time

A second pullback can offer another continuation entry, but the stock is now further into its trend. More holders have profits, expectations are higher, and the probability of maturity is increasing. Therefore, the second pullback must be judged more strictly than the first.

The weekly 8 EMA can become important because a successful trend often rides that average through intermediate pauses. The strongest setup appears when the weekly average aligns with a major horizontal level such as the earnings-gap area or prior breakout. The line alone is not a signal; price must reject the area and recover with strength.

MRVL illustrates the complete concept: a meaningful earnings event, an initial successful continuation, and a later retreat into a key level plus weekly 8 EMA. The opportunity came from confluence, lower supply, and rejection—not from mechanically buying a stepped line.

## Real example or short trading story

MRVL later returned toward a major key level while the weekly 8 EMA was rising into the same area.

This was not “buy the weekly 8 EMA.” The setup required the existing trend, controlled contraction, the key level, and a clear rejection that showed demand returning.

The second pullback qualified because the full structure remained healthy—not because a moving average was touched.

### The MRVL example

It mattered because the catalyst, trend, horizontal support, volume contraction, and rejection aligned.

## Key rules / steps

1. Confirm that the first pullback worked and the stock produced another healthy expansion.
2. Require relative strength and group leadership to remain intact.
3. Judge whether the weekly trend is orderly or becoming climactic.
4. Map the weekly 8 EMA together with horizontal support.
5. Demand a clear rejection, reclaim, or higher low.
6. Reduce interest when each pullback becomes deeper, wider, or heavier in volume.

**Rule to remember**

> **The later the pullback, the higher the standard.**

## Common mistakes

- Buying a falling stock simply because it touched a moving average
- Assuming every weekly 8 EMA touch is support
- Applying first-pullback standards to a mature third or fourth pullback
- Ignoring declining RS during the later retreat
- Buying before the stock proves it can reclaim the level

### Coach's note

Watch for **fear during weakness**. A pullback naturally feels less comfortable than a breakout. The answer is not blind courage; it is objective evidence of low supply and returning demand.
