# Separate Process From Outcome

## Why this matters

A good trade can lose and a bad trade can win. Grade the quality of the decision separately from the financial result.

## Clear explanation

Your journal turns experience into evidence. Record the decision honestly, not just the profit or loss.

Trading outcomes are noisy. A poor decision can make money, and a professional decision can lose.

### Four categories

### Good process, profitable outcome

The ideal result. Study what can be repeated.

### Good process, losing outcome

A normal business expense. Confirm that the setup and execution followed the rules.

### Poor process, profitable outcome

The most dangerous category because profit rewards bad behavior. Label the violation clearly.

### Poor process, losing outcome

A preventable loss. Identify the earliest decision error.

### Execution grade

Score:

- market alignment
- stock quality
- setup quality
- entry efficiency
- risk compliance
- management
- emotional discipline

Keep the execution grade separate from P&L.

### Rule weighting

Critical risk rules should carry more weight than minor process rules. A trade that exceeded maximum risk cannot receive an A grade because the setup looked good.

### How to apply it in real time

A profitable rule violation is dangerous because it teaches the brain that the violation worked. A disciplined loss is useful because it confirms that the process can be followed under uncertainty. Therefore, every trade receives two separate grades: outcome and execution quality.

Execution quality should include market fit, stock selection, setup validity, entry efficiency, risk compliance, and management. The score should be determined by rules known before the outcome. A +4R chase above the LOD threshold can receive a poor process grade.

A -1R clean breakout that failed within the planned stop can receive a high grade.

Over a large sample, good process should improve expectancy. If high-quality trades consistently lose, the system or market classification requires study. Separating process from outcome does not mean ignoring performance; it prevents one result from corrupting the diagnosis.

## Real example or short trading story

One trade loses 1R after every rule was followed. Another earns 3R even though the entry was late and oversized.

If you grade only the outcome, you punish the good process and reward the bad one.

You record two separate scores: **financial result** and **execution quality**. Over a large sample, that separation shows which behavior deserves to be repeated.

## Key rules / steps

1. Grade setup quality and execution before looking at final P&L when possible.
2. Separate market, selection, entry, risk, and management scores.
3. Identify profitable violations as high-priority mistakes.
4. Treat planned losses as normal operating expenses.
5. Compare expectancy by process grade over meaningful samples.
6. Change rules only when data and chart review support the change.

**Rule to remember**

> **Do not let one outcome rewrite a sound rule.**

## Common mistakes

- Recording only winners and losses instead of recording the decision process
- Calling every winner a good trade
- Calling every loss a strategy failure
- Using “discipline” to avoid examining poor expectancy
- Changing the grade because the stock later recovered after the stop

### Coach's note

Watch for **ego and self-deception**. A journal is useless when it protects your self-image. Honest review turns emotion into data and data into a specific correction.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Blind-review thirty trades by hiding the final result and grading only the pre-entry and management evidence. Reveal the outcomes afterward. Create four groups: good win, good loss, bad win, bad loss.

The bad-win group should receive the most attention because it contains behavior likely to be repeated.

### Reflection

1. What did the decision quality reveal that the profit or loss did not?
2. Which repeated behavior should become the next measurable process goal?
3. What evidence will you record so the review cannot be rewritten by hindsight?
