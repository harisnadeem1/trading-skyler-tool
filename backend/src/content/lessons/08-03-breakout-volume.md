# Breakout Volume, Reversal Volume, and Failure

## Why this matters

When price attempts to resume the trend, participation should improve. Weak volume on a breakout is less convincing; heavy volume on failure is a warning.

## Clear explanation

Volume shows how much participation stands behind price. Read it with location and structure; never treat one volume bar as a complete signal.

When a stock transitions from contraction to expansion, demand should become more visible. **You wait for price to hold and participation to improve.**

### Breakout volume

A healthy breakout often shows:

- relative volume above the normal pace
- increasing activity as price clears the pivot
- strong closing location
- limited upper wick
- follow-through in subsequent sessions

### Reversal volume

For a controlled pullback, the first reversal candle may begin on moderate volume. More important is whether volume expands as price reclaims the pivot and holds support.

### Low-volume breakout

A low-volume breakout can still work in a very tight, low-supply base, but it deserves more caution. Ask whether price is separating cleanly or merely drifting above the pivot.

### High-volume failure

Warning signs:

- breakout volume is high but price closes weakly
- price falls back into the base
- a large upper wick forms
- the next day breaks the breakout low
- RS fails to confirm

High participation with poor price progress can mean supply absorbed the demand.

### Volume after entry

Constructive continuation shows volume expanding on advances and contracting on pullbacks. Repeated heavy down-volume bars can indicate distribution even before the final trend break.

### How to apply it in real time

When a stock attempts to leave a base or end a pullback, volume should support the claim that demand is returning. The required amount varies. A compact breakout from an extremely dry base may work with steady accumulation rather than a spectacular spike.

A move through major long-term resistance may need stronger participation.

Intraday relative volume is especially useful. Evaluate whether the current pace is above normal for that time of day and whether it improves as price approaches the trigger. A breakout whose volume fades while price stalls is less convincing.

Failure volume matters as much as success volume. A breakout that reverses back through the pivot on heavy volume shows supply. A pullback reversal that cannot attract demand may remain only a bounce.

Volume should confirm the direction in which price is trying to leave the structure.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock clears its pivot early in the session. The price move looks good, but relative volume is weak and the stock cannot separate from the level.

Later, selling volume expands as price falls back into the base. That is different from a breakout that attracts participation and holds above the pivot.

Volume confirms or questions the price move. It never replaces the setup itself.

## Key rules / steps

1. Judge the volume requirement relative to the importance of the level.
2. Use time-of-day run rate for intraday decisions.
3. Look for volume expansion as price clears or reclaims the trigger.
4. Watch whether volume produces price progress and a strong close.
5. Monitor the first test after breakout for low-volume support versus heavy rejection.
6. Reclassify quickly when failure occurs on abnormal selling volume.

**Rule to remember**

> **Volume should confirm what price is trying to do.**

## Common mistakes

- Reading volume color without considering price direction and location
- Demanding a rigid 150% volume threshold for every stock and time
- Ignoring weakening run rate because price remains slightly above the pivot
- Celebrating high volume that produces no upward progress
- Calling a low-volume dead-cat bounce a confirmed reversal

### Coach's note

Watch for **indicator fixation**. Volume labels can feel authoritative. They remain evidence that must be interpreted with price, location, and market context.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Use replay on ten breakouts and ten pullback reversals. At 30-minute intervals, record relative volume run rate and price progress from the trigger. Identify where strong volume failed to produce progress and where modest volume was sufficient because supply was exceptionally dry.

### Reflection

1. What price-and-volume sequence is the chart showing?
2. Could the same volume bar mean something different in another location?
3. What participation would you need to see before treating continuation as confirmed?
