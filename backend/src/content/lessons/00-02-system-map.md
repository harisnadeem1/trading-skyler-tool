# The Complete Decision Map

## Why this matters

Every trade follows the same order: market first, stock second, pattern third, trigger fourth, review last. Skipping the order is how random trades enter the process.

## Clear explanation

You are building a decision process, not collecting isolated tricks. Learn the order first. The details only become useful when they fit that order.

The Ronin Trading System is best understood as a sequence of gates. A stock does not become actionable because one attractive feature is present. It must pass through each gate in order.

### Gate 1: Market condition

First, determine whether the environment is supportive of long swing trades. Index position matters, but the behavior of leading stocks matters even more. Ask:

- Are breakouts holding above their pivots?
- Are controlled pullbacks finding support?
- Are new highs expanding or failing?
- Are my strongest watchlist names acting well?
- Are existing positions producing follow-through?

The market does not need to be perfect, but it must not be repeatedly punishing the exact behavior I trade.

### Gate 2: Leadership

The stock must show evidence of leadership. I want strong relative performance, a constructive weekly chart, sufficient liquidity, and a reason institutions may care. A weak stock cannot be repaired by a beautiful rectangle.

Leadership often appears before the setup through:

- an earnings gap
- a highest-volume or HV1 event
- a move into new highs
- strength while the index is weak
- rapid recovery after a market correction
- clear leadership within a strong industry group

### Gate 3: Pattern classification

The daily chart must fit one of two categories:

### Flat Base Breakout
A strong stock holds near its highs, trades sideways, contracts in range and volume, and develops a clear upper pivot.

### Controlled Pullback
A proven leader retreats toward a logical support area on controlled price action and then shows that demand is returning.

If you cannot classify the chart clearly, it is not ready.

### Gate 4: Key level and invalidation

Before thinking about the trigger, identify the area that should hold if the thesis is correct. This can be a prior breakout, earnings-gap support, the 8 or 21 EMA combined with price structure, a daily higher low, or the weekly 8 EMA.

The level must exist before the trade. Do not invent support after price starts falling.

### Gate 5: Contraction

The stock should become quieter before it becomes actionable. Look for:

- smaller candles
- tighter closes
- lower volume
- stable support
- fewer wide downside bars
- volatility contraction near the pivot or support area

Contraction is the evidence that supply is being absorbed.

### Gate 6: Confirmation

Confirmation shows that contraction may be ending. Examples include:

- a breakout through a clear pivot
- a higher low and intraday pivot
- a reclaim of support
- a strong reversal candle
- renewed relative volume
- a close near the high of the day
- leadership on a weak market session

The location creates the opportunity. Confirmation creates the trade.

### Gate 7: Entry efficiency

A valid setup can still be entered poorly. The 30-minute chart helps determine whether the entry is close enough to the low of day and the structural stop. The Ronin framework prefers entries with a low-of-day distance below roughly **60% of daily ATR** and an entry-to-stop distance generally no greater than approximately **one daily ATR**.

These are efficiency filters, not predictions.

### Gate 8: Management and review

Once the position is open, price determines whether the thesis remains valid. A failed breakout that falls back into the base is different from a healthy trend that pulls back normally. After the trade closes, review both the result and the process.

A profitable rule violation is still poor execution. A correctly executed loss can still be a professional trade.

### The full flow

> Market → Leader → Setup → Key level → Contraction → Confirmation → Efficient entry → Management → Review

When a gate fails, stop. Do not compensate for a weak market with a tighter stop, or for a poor stock with a stronger story. The strength of the system comes from alignment, not from any single indicator.

### How to apply it in real time

A top-down process prevents a beautiful lower-timeframe pattern from distracting you from weak context. The sequence starts with the market because long swing trades depend on follow-through. It then moves to stock quality, catalyst, higher-timeframe structure, daily setup, and finally the entry trigger.

Reversing this sequence produces the common mistake of finding an exciting intraday candle first and inventing reasons to justify it afterward.

Think of the decision map as a chain. Each link answers a different question. Market regime asks whether risk should be taken at all.

Stock selection asks whether this is the kind of vehicle capable of producing a meaningful move. The weekly chart asks whether overhead supply and trend maturity are acceptable. The daily chart asks whether a valid parent setup exists.

The 30-minute chart asks whether buyers are returning at an efficient location. Risk and management rules determine whether the idea can be expressed responsibly.

A weak answer at an early stage cannot be repaired by a strong answer later. Excellent volume cannot fix a loose base. A tight intraday pivot cannot fix a broken weekly trend.

A compelling story cannot fix weak relative strength.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock is near its highs and begins moving through an intraday pivot. It looks exciting, but the broader market is Defensive, the daily base is loose, and the Low-of-Day distance is already extended.

The decision map stops the trade before emotion takes over. The trigger exists, but several earlier gates have failed.

This is why the gates stay in order. A late-stage trigger cannot repair weak context, weak structure, or poor entry efficiency.

## Key rules / steps

1. Market regime: Aggressive, Constructive, Defensive, or No Trade.
2. Leadership: price, relative strength, group behavior, liquidity, and catalyst.
3. Weekly context: trend, major resistance, weekly 8 EMA, and maturity.
4. Daily structure: one of the two parent setups with clear key levels.
5. Tactical trigger: 30-minute higher low, reclaim, pivot, or opening compression.
6. Execution and review: planned risk, management, screenshots, and process grade.

**Rule to remember**

> **Never use a lower-timeframe trigger to justify a daily setup that does not exist.**

## Common mistakes

- Trying to memorize everything at once instead of learning the decision order
- Starting with the 30-minute chart because it looks actionable
- Skipping the market regime after a stock appears “too strong to fail.”
- Allowing a fundamental story to replace price confirmation
- Changing the sequence after a losing streak

### Coach's note

Watch for **uncertainty and responsibility**. The mind looks for certainty, but trading offers probabilities. Your job is to own the quality of the decision without demanding a particular outcome.
