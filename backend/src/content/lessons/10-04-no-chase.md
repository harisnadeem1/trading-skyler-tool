# The No-Chase Protocol

## Why this matters

Missing a move is not a loss. When price leaves without a clean entry, wait for a new base, pullback, or intraday structure.

## Clear explanation

A 30-minute trigger is valid only after the higher-timeframe setup is complete. Speed does not repair weak structure.

Missing a move is emotionally uncomfortable, but chasing converts a planned setup into an unplanned trade.

### Signs the entry is gone

- LOD ATR exceeds the preferred range
- price is far above the daily pivot
- entry-to-stop distance is excessive
- the breakout candle is already unusually large
- price is extended from the 8 EMA
- the stock is approaching obvious resistance
- volume is climax-like rather than controlled

### The protocol

### Step 1: Do nothing

Do not enter because of urgency.

### Step 2: Set a new alert

Possible future structures:

- retest of the breakout pivot
- first controlled pullback
- inside day
- 30-minute higher low
- new flat base

### Step 3: Remove emotional language

Replace “I missed it” with “the planned entry is no longer available.” This keeps the decision factual.

### Step 4: Review the plan

If the stock repeatedly leaves without you, your trigger may be too strict. Change rules only after reviewing a meaningful sample, not during the live move.

### Opportunity cost

The market produces new setups continuously. One missed trade does not damage the business. A chased trade can damage both capital and discipline.

### How to apply it in real time

A missed entry creates one of the most dangerous emotional states in momentum trading. The stock confirms the thesis, but the entry is no longer efficient. The temptation is to buy because being right feels more important than executing well.

The No-Chase Protocol converts that emotion into a predefined decision tree.

First, recalculate LOD ATR and entry-to-stop ATR. If the entry remains within rules and a compact trigger still exists, the trade may still be valid. If the stock is extended, do nothing.

Watch for a new 30-minute base, an orderly first pullback, or a later daily structure. If none forms, let the stock go.

A stock can be an excellent leader and a terrible entry at the same time. The system measures success by whether you followed the plan, not by whether the stock later made a large move without them.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock clears the pivot and moves quickly beyond your planned entry. The chart still looks strong, which makes chasing feel reasonable.

You check the distance to the Low of Day, the structural stop, and the moving averages. The entry is gone.

You set an alert for a new structure and move on. Strength does not make any price acceptable.

## Key rules / steps

1. Pause before acting and recalculate objective distance metrics.
2. Identify whether a new compact structure has formed or price is simply vertical.
3. Never move the stop upward to manufacture acceptable risk.
4. Add the stock to a first-pullback watchlist if the breakout is missed.
5. Screenshot the missed trade and record whether the original alert or preparation failed.
6. Accept that no single trade is required for long-term success.

**Rule to remember**

> **An entry is either efficient or it is gone.**

## Common mistakes

- Using the trigger to justify chasing an already extended entry
- Buying a smaller position and pretending the entry quality no longer matters
- Using a one-minute pause as a new base
- Revenge-watching the stock and missing better prepared setups
- Changing the chase threshold after seeing the future result

### Coach's note

Watch for **hesitation and impulse**. A planned trigger can produce fear before entry and urgency after it moves. Rehearsed rules reduce both freezing and chasing.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Create a “missed trade journal” for twenty examples. Record why the entry was missed, the LOD ATR at the moment you noticed it, whether a second structure formed, and the correct action. Grade the decision, not the later price.

This transforms FOMO into operational feedback.

### Reflection

1. What is the exact trigger, and which higher-timeframe setup gives it meaning?
2. At what price would the entry become too extended under the Ronin rules?
3. What pre-entry step will stop hesitation or impulse from changing the plan?
