# When the Trade Is Working

## Why this matters

A working trade separates from the entry, holds structure, and creates new higher lows. Give it room only while the chart continues to earn that room.

## Clear explanation

Once you are in the trade, manage the chart—not your fear of losing profit and not your need to be right.

A profitable trade should remain open while the trend and structure stay healthy.

### Healthy behavior

- higher highs and higher lows
- support at the 8 or 21 EMA
- pullbacks on lower volume
- strong closes after tests
- RS remaining high
- no repeated distribution
- group leadership continuing

### Do not sell only because profit exists

A gain is not a sell signal. The system aims to capture the central part of a trend, not the exact top.

### Partial profit-taking

A trim may be appropriate when:

- price becomes vertical
- the stock is extended from support
- volume becomes climax-like
- a major target or resistance is reached
- the position has grown too large relative to the portfolio

A 5R gain can be a reference point, but it is not an automatic exit. The quality of the move matters.

### Breakeven stops

Do not move the stop to breakeven only because you fear losing a profit. Move it when a new structural level justifies the change. An automatic breakeven stop can remove you during a normal pullback.

### How to apply it in real time

A winning trade often becomes emotionally harder as the profit grows. You want to protect the gain and begins treating every normal pullback as a threat. The system avoids this by defining healthy behavior: higher highs and higher lows, support at the 8 or 21 EMA, constructive volume, strong RS, and no major distribution.

The amount of profit does not determine whether the trend is intact. A +2R trade can still be early, and a +6R trade can still be healthy. Conversely, a small gain can deserve exit if the breakout fails.

Management should follow price structure and the speed of the stock.

Your job is to capture the central portion of a trend, not the exact top. This requires giving a healthy leader enough room to fluctuate while progressively reducing risk when new higher lows or support structures form.

## Real example or short trading story

A position moves higher, holds the 8 EMA, and forms orderly higher lows. You have a profit, but the trend remains healthy.

Selling only because the number is green would replace structure with emotion.

You stay with the trade while the chosen management method remains valid, trimming only when the plan and extension justify it.

## Key rules / steps

1. Identify the moving average or higher-low sequence controlling the current trend.
2. Compare pullback volume with expansion volume.
3. Monitor RS and closing quality.
4. Distinguish normal consolidation from a failed breakout or distribution cluster.
5. Advance the stop only when new structure justifies it.
6. Keep the original thesis and current management plan visible in the journal.

**Rule to remember**

> **Do not sell strength simply because the position is profitable.**

## Common mistakes

- Managing the unrealized P&L instead of managing the chart
- Selling because the profit amount feels large
- Moving to breakeven before the stock has built new support
- Using the same trailing method for every speed of trend
- Ignoring exhaustion because the company story remains exciting

### Coach's note

Watch for **greed, fear, and the disposition effect**. You may sell winners too early and hold failures too long. Structure must determine management, not the desire to feel safe or avoid regret.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Choose ten large winners and recreate the chart one day at a time. Mark every point where a structure-based hold was justified and every point where fear would have suggested selling. Compare the cumulative difference.

Then study losing trades to ensure the same patience would not have ignored failure.

### Reflection

1. What chart behavior—not profit or fear—would change your management decision?
2. Which trailing or trimming method was chosen before the trade became emotional?
3. How will you distinguish a normal pullback from real structural failure?
