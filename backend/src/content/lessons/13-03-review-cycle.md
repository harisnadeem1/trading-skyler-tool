# Daily, Weekly, and Monthly Review Cycle

## Why this matters

Daily review catches execution errors, weekly review finds repeated behavior, and monthly review evaluates the system over a meaningful sample.

## Clear explanation

Your journal turns experience into evidence. Record the decision honestly, not just the profit or loss.

Review converts experience into improvement.

### Daily review

Ask:

- Did I follow the market plan?
- Did I force a trade?
- Did I chase?
- Were stops structural?
- Did I respect risk?
- What did the market reward?
- What should change tomorrow?

### Weekly review

Summarize:

- total R
- best and worst execution
- most common violation
- setup performance
- average LOD ATR %
- market-regime alignment
- missed high-quality setup
- one behavior to repeat
- one behavior to eliminate

### Monthly review

Analyze:

- equity and drawdown curves
- expectancy
- Flat Base versus Controlled Pullback results
- subtype results
- performance by regime
- rule-followed versus rule-broken trades
- sample size
- performance by RS, ADR, catalyst, and entry efficiency

### Sample-size protection

Do not redesign the system after four trades. Label conclusions as provisional until the sample is meaningful. Twenty or more trades in a category provide a more useful starting point.

### Process goals

Choose behavioral goals, such as:

- no entries above 60% LOD ATR
- complete every pre-trade screenshot
- no trades outside the two parent setups
- reduce activity in Defensive regimes

Avoid goals that depend entirely on market outcomes.

### How to apply it in real time

Review must operate at several speeds. The daily review catches immediate execution errors while memory is fresh. The weekly review identifies repeated behavior and market-regime mismatch.

The monthly review evaluates expectancy, drawdown, setup performance, and whether rules are producing the intended edge.

Each review should end with a small number of actions. “Be more disciplined” is too vague. “No entries above 60% LOD ATR next week” is measurable.

Process goals are more useful than profit targets because they remain under your control.

Sample size protects against overreaction. A setup with four trades cannot support a strong conclusion. Display the number of observations beside every statistic and combine quantitative results with chart review.

A negative month does not automatically invalidate the system, and a strong month does not prove every rule is optimal.

## Real example or short trading story

At the end of a profitable week, you notice that two winners came from chased entries. Without review, the profit would hide the problem.

The daily review captures the facts. The weekly review finds repeated behavior. The monthly review checks whether the pattern is real across a larger sample.

Review turns isolated trades into evidence and evidence into a better operating plan.

## Key rules / steps

1. Daily: review plan adherence, entries, stops, emotional state, and missing screenshots.
2. Weekly: summarize total R, process grades, common violations, and market fit.
3. Monthly: analyze expectancy, drawdown, setup, regime, LOD ATR, and management.
4. Choose one measurable process objective for the next period.
5. Keep a change log for any rule modification.
6. Retest changes over a defined sample rather than switching immediately.

**Rule to remember**

> **Review on a schedule, not only after painful losses.**

## Common mistakes

- Recording only winners and losses instead of recording the decision process
- Reviewing only after a losing period
- Creating ten goals and following none
- Drawing conclusions from tiny samples
- Optimizing rules to the best historical winners

### Coach's note

Watch for **ego and self-deception**. A journal is useless when it protects your self-image. Honest review turns emotion into data and data into a specific correction.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Complete a monthly review using at least five breakdowns: setup, regime, process grade, LOD ATR bucket, and exit reason. For each breakdown, show sample size. Choose one behavior to reinforce and one to eliminate.

Define exactly how next month’s journal will measure both.

### Reflection

1. What did the decision quality reveal that the profit or loss did not?
2. Which repeated behavior should become the next measurable process goal?
3. What evidence will you record so the review cannot be rewritten by hindsight?
