# Use the Community Without Losing Independence

## Why this matters

A practical posting and review routine that uses feedback to improve skill rather than copy trades.

## Clear explanation

The private community is a study room, not a signal room. Use it to improve your thinking without giving away your responsibility.

A trading community is useful when it improves observation, preparation, and review. It becomes harmful when members copy trades or wait for someone else to make decisions.

The Ronin Discord should be used as a structured study room.

### A productive daily routine

### Before the market

Post only when the idea is already planned. Include:

- market regime
- stock and industry leadership
- parent setup
- key level
- confirmation required
- invalidation
- whether the stock is near earnings

### During the market

Avoid narrating every candle. Update only when the planned condition changes:

- the trigger appears
- the setup invalidates
- the stock becomes too extended
- market conditions materially change

### After the market

Review the decision using screenshots. Explain:

- what you expected
- what actually happened
- whether the rules were followed
- what you would repeat
- what must change

### How to ask a good question

Weak question:

> “Is this a buy?”

Better question:

> “The weekly trend is constructive and the daily chart is forming a shallow flat base near highs. Volume is contracting, but the pivot is still loose. Which rule is missing before this becomes actionable?”

The better question produces a transferable lesson.

### How to give useful feedback

- Refer to a course rule
- Describe the chart behavior rather than the outcome
- Explain uncertainty honestly
- Do not shame losses or celebrate reckless wins
- Ask the member to identify the invalidation and missing evidence

### Protect the community

Premium content, member screenshots, and private discussions remain inside the group. Do not repost another member’s work without permission.

### Final community checklist

- [ ] I can explain my own trade before reading other opinions
- [ ] I ask for feedback on process, not a signal
- [ ] I do not copy position size or entries
- [ ] I post both winners and failures
- [ ] I review rules instead of defending ego
- [ ] I leave with a clearer independent decision process

## Real example or short trading story

A member asks, “Is this a buy?” The question gives away the decision.

A better post says: “The weekly trend is constructive, the daily chart is forming a shallow flat base near highs, and volume is contracting, but the pivot remains loose. Which rule is missing before this becomes actionable?”

That question produces a lesson you can reuse. The community should improve how you think, not tell you what to do.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Write your own plan before reading other opinions.**
2. **Ask questions about rules and missing evidence, not whether to buy or sell.**
3. **Give feedback from the course language and chart behavior.**
4. **Review both winners and failures without defending ego.**
5. **Leave every discussion with a stronger independent process.**

**Rule to remember**

> The best community does not tell you what to think. It teaches you how to examine your thinking.

## Common mistakes

- Asking others to make the buy or sell decision
- Reading opinions before writing your own plan
- Copying another member’s entry, stop, or position size

### Coach's note

Watch for **social proof and herd behavior**. Community can improve learning or weaken independence. Write your own plan before reading other opinions.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Create a chart-study post using a historical trade. Write your plan before reading any member comments.

After receiving feedback, record which course rule became clearer. Do not change the historical plan to make the outcome look better.

### Reflection

1. How will you use the community without giving away your decision-making responsibility?
2. What would a high-quality process question look like for this lesson?
3. What personal plan must exist before you read another member’s opinion?
