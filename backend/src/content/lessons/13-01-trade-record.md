# The Complete Trade Record

## Why this matters

A useful journal records the market, stock, setup, entry, stop, screenshots, management, and lesson. P&L alone does not explain behavior.

## Clear explanation

Your journal turns experience into evidence. Record the decision honestly, not just the profit or loss.

A journal should capture the decision, not only the profit or loss.

### Before entry

Record:

- market regime
- stock and group
- catalyst
- setup type and subtype
- weekly thesis
- daily structure
- key level
- pivot
- volume condition
- RS condition
- planned entry and stop
- daily ATR
- LOD ATR %
- entry-to-stop ATR
- planned risk and position size
- screenshots

### During the trade

Record:

- fills
- adds and trims
- stop changes
- structural changes
- emotional reactions
- deviations from the plan

### After exit

Record:

- result in currency and R
- R based on maximum committed risk
- MFE and MAE
- exit reason
- rule-compliance score
- lesson
- whether you would take the same trade again

### Preserve the original plan

Do not edit the pre-trade thesis after the outcome. The journal must show what you believed before the trade.

### Screenshots

Save weekly, daily, and 30-minute charts before entry, plus exit and post-trade charts. Visual review builds pattern recognition faster than numbers alone.

### How to apply it in real time

A useful journal captures the information available before entry, the sequence of decisions during the trade, and the review after exit. If the plan is edited after the outcome, the record loses its value. Preserve original screenshots, planned levels, market regime, and thesis separately from post-trade commentary.

The record should allow you to reconstruct why the stock qualified: leadership, catalyst, weekly context, daily setup, volume, key level, and trigger. It should also include objective execution fields such as LOD ATR, entry-to-stop ATR, risk percentage, position size, and fills.

During the trade, log adds, trims, stop changes, and the reason for each. Afterward, grade the process, record MFE and MAE when available, and identify one repeatable lesson. A long emotional diary is less useful than precise evidence tied to rules.

## Real example or short trading story

You remember a trade as disciplined because it became a winner. The screenshots show something different: the entry was chased, the stop was moved, and the profit came from luck.

Memory protects the ego. A complete trade record protects the process.

You preserve the original plan, every execution, the chart before and after, the rule results, and the lesson while the facts are still clear.

## Key rules / steps

1. Before entry: market regime, stock thesis, setup, catalyst, key levels, screenshots, and plan.
2. At entry: fills, stop, ATR metrics, volume, size, and confirmation.
3. During trade: adds, trims, stop changes, news, and management rationale.
4. At exit: fills, R result, exit reason, MFE, MAE, and chart.
5. After trade: rule checklist, process grade, mistake category, and lesson.
6. Keep the original plan immutable or versioned.

**Rule to remember**

> **Record enough information to reconstruct the decision months later.**

## Common mistakes

- Recording only winners and losses instead of recording the decision process
- Writing the thesis after the result is known
- Using vague labels such as “bad trade” without naming the rule
- Collecting data that never changes review or behavior

### Coach's note

Watch for **ego and self-deception**. A journal is useless when it protects your self-image. Honest review turns emotion into data and data into a specific correction.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Create a minimum viable trade template and complete it for the last twenty trades. Highlight fields that were impossible to reconstruct after the fact; those belong in the pre-trade workflow. Remove fields that produced no useful comparison after twenty samples.

### Reflection

1. What did the decision quality reveal that the profit or loss did not?
2. Which repeated behavior should become the next measurable process goal?
3. What evidence will you record so the review cannot be rewritten by hindsight?
