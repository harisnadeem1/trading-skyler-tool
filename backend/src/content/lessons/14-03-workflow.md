# The Complete Weekly and Daily Workflow

## Why this matters

The weekly workflow builds the list; the daily workflow updates conditions and levels; the intraday workflow waits for confirmation.

## Clear explanation

This module connects the entire system into one operating process. You should be able to explain every decision before you take it.

### Weekend

1. Run momentum, earnings-gap, and HVE scans.
2. Review weekly charts first.
3. Remove damaged or illiquid stocks.
4. Classify each candidate as Flat Base, Controlled Pullback, or Developing.
5. Mark key levels, pivots, support, and earnings dates.
6. Build a focused master list.
7. Save clean chart screenshots.

### Each evening

1. Update the Trend Map.
2. Review index structure and leader behavior.
3. Promote or demote watchlist names.
4. Calculate ATR and planned levels.
5. Set alerts.
6. Write the next-day focus list.

### Before the open

1. Check news and gaps.
2. Remove plans made invalid by overnight price.
3. Review market regime.
4. Confirm no unplanned earnings risk.
5. Prepare 30-minute trigger scenarios.

### First 30 minutes

1. Observe opening behavior.
2. Track relative volume and low of day.
3. Wait for a clear pivot, higher low, or reclaim.
4. Recalculate LOD ATR and entry-to-stop ATR.
5. Execute only if the entire decision map remains aligned.

### After the close

1. Save screenshots.
2. Update fills and notes.
3. Grade execution.
4. Review market feedback.
5. Prepare tomorrow without carrying emotional conclusions.

### How to apply it in real time

A complete workflow removes the need to improvise every morning. Weekend preparation builds the leadership universe and marks developing structures. End-of-day work updates the watchlist, market regime, and alerts.

Premarket review handles new gaps and news. The opening period provides evidence; it does not erase the plan.

Scanning should occur on weekly and daily charts. The weekly review identifies long-term leaders and eliminates overhead supply. The daily review classifies the two parent setups.

Actionable names receive a written plan including trigger, support, invalidation, ATR metrics, earnings date, and market requirements.

During the session, alerts direct attention. After entry, management follows the predefined structure. After the close, screenshots and notes are completed while memory is fresh.

The weekly and monthly review closes the loop.

## Real example or short trading story

On the weekend, you build the master list and map the important levels. Each evening, you promote the best developing setups. Before the open, you review gaps and market conditions.

During the first 30 minutes, you observe instead of forcing action. After the close, you journal and update the list.

The workflow moves decisions away from emotion and into scheduled preparation.

## Key rules / steps

1. Weekend: run momentum and catalyst scans, review weekly charts, and build leadership lists.
2. Each evening: update regime, daily structures, levels, ATR, and alerts.
3. Premarket: check gaps, news, earnings, and whether the plan remains valid.
4. Opening 10–30 minutes: observe relative strength, low of day, volume run rate, and trigger formation.
5. Execution: calculate size and act only when the complete plan aligns.
6. Post-market: journal, review, and prepare the next session.
7. Weekend review: analyze process, not just P&L.

**Rule to remember**

> **Preparation should happen before urgency appears.**

## Common mistakes

- Using a checklist mechanically without understanding why each item exists
- Rebuilding the watchlist from social media every morning
- Staring at all charts instead of using alerts
- Allowing premarket movement to create an unplanned chase
- Skipping review after a profitable week

### Coach's note

Watch for **consistency under pressure**. Mastery means the same decision sequence is used when excited, afraid, bored, winning, or losing.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Run the complete workflow in simulation for four weeks. Track time spent in scanning, planning, execution, and review. Remove repetitive steps that do not improve decisions, but do not remove checks simply because they prevent trades.

The final workflow should be sustainable during both active and quiet markets.
