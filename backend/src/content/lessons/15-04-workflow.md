# Read the Complete Ronin Chart

## Why this matters

Combine the indicators into one repeatable weekly, daily, and 30-minute workflow without turning them into automatic signals.

## Clear explanation

Your TradingView workspace should make the Ronin process easier to see. Indicators support the decision; they do not make the decision.

A chart full of information is useful only when you read it in a fixed order. The Ronin order prevents one exciting indicator value from overriding the setup.

> **Read price first. Use indicators to confirm or measure what price is already showing.**

### Step 1: Read the weekly structure

Before studying an entry, answer:

- Is price above a rising 200 SMA and healthy major trend references?
- Is the stock close to highs or below heavy overhead supply?
- Is the weekly 8 EMA rising?
- Is the trend early enough to have room, or already wide and mature?

If the weekly chart is damaged, stop. Do not search the 30-minute chart for a reason to buy.

### Step 2: Classify the daily setup

Choose only one label:

- **Flat Base Breakout**
- **Controlled Pullback**
- **No Setup**

Then mark the pivot, support, key level, and invalidation area.

### Step 3: Read the moving-average hierarchy

A constructive long setup usually has the shorter averages above the longer averages and rising. More important than perfect order is whether price is respecting the areas logically.

- 8 EMA: immediate momentum
- 21 EMA: intermediate support
- weekly 8 EMA: higher-timeframe support
- 50 EMA: broader trend
- 200 SMA: long-term structure

### Step 4: Read volume

Ask:

- Was there strong participation during the prior expansion?
- Is volume generally quieter during the current contraction?
- Are heavy selling days limited?
- Is volume improving as price confirms?

A high-volume label creates context. It does not create a trade by itself.

### Step 5: Read leadership and liquidity

Use the Swing Data and RS tools to confirm:

- sufficient ADR%
- sufficient average dollar volume
- strong RS Rating or RS line
- favorable sector or group behavior
- reasonable Up/Down Volume ratio

Reject the chart when liquidity is poor or the stock is no longer acting like a leader.

### Step 6: Move to the 30-minute chart

Wait for one of the four triggers:

1. higher low;
2. pivot breakout;
3. reclaim;
4. opening-range compression.

Then check:

- Low-of-Day distance is preferably below about 60% of daily ATR
- the logical stop is still reasonably close
- live relative or projected volume supports the move
- the entry is not far above the daily pivot or support

### Step 7: Check extension

Use ATR% multiple from 50-MA to understand whether the stock is in a normal trend or a very extended phase. This does not veto every entry, but it changes the quality of the opportunity and how much room may remain.

### Example: Flat Base Breakout

- Weekly trend healthy
- Daily stock holds near highs
- Final third of the base tightens
- Volume dries up
- Pivot is obvious
- 30-minute price compresses under the pivot
- Low-of-Day distance remains efficient
- Volume improves as price clears the level

### Example: Controlled Pullback

- Prior expansion proves demand
- Pullback reaches the 8 EMA, 21 EMA, weekly 8 EMA, or prior breakout area
- Volume contracts
- Price forms a higher low or reclaim
- RS remains firm
- 30-minute trigger confirms demand is returning

### The final screen checklist

- [ ] Market condition supports long swing trades
- [ ] Stock is a leader with sufficient movement and liquidity
- [ ] Daily setup is one of the two parent patterns
- [ ] Weekly chart supports the idea
- [ ] Volume behavior matches the stage of the pattern
- [ ] Key level and invalidation are obvious
- [ ] 30-minute trigger confirms the daily setup
- [ ] Low-of-Day and stop distance remain efficient
- [ ] Entry is not chased

## Real example or short trading story

You open a candidate and read it in the same order every time: weekly structure, daily setup, moving-average hierarchy, volume, leadership and liquidity, 30-minute trigger, then extension.

Because the order is fixed, one exciting candle cannot dominate the whole decision.

The chart becomes a structured conversation: context first, setup second, execution last.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Read the weekly structure.** Confirm trend, resistance, and maturity.
2. **Classify the daily setup.** It must be a Flat Base Breakout or Controlled Pullback.
3. **Read the moving-average hierarchy.** Identify short-, intermediate-, and long-term support.
4. **Read volume, leadership, and liquidity.**
5. **Move to the 30-minute chart for the trigger.**
6. **Check Low-of-Day and entry-to-stop extension.**
7. **Reject the trade when any critical gate fails.**

**Rule to remember**

> A professional workspace does not make the decision for you. It makes the right evidence difficult to ignore.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the 30-minute chart
- Letting one indicator override weak higher-timeframe structure
- Skipping extension checks because the setup looks strong

### Coach's note

Watch for **tool dependency**. A clean workspace improves attention, but no indicator can replace market context, price structure, or independent judgment.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Choose ten historical candidates and read each chart in the seven-step order taught in this lesson. Hide future candles.

For every chart, write the weekly context, daily setup, moving-average hierarchy, volume message, leadership/liquidity status, 30-minute trigger, and extension decision. Reject any chart where one critical gate fails.
