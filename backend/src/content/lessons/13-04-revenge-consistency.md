# Revenge Trading, Consistency, and the Reset Protocol

## Why this matters

Revenge trading is not always obvious. It may look like a larger position, a lower-quality setup, a rushed re-entry, or the refusal to end a bad session. The common purpose is to erase an uncomfortable result quickly.

## Clear explanation

Your journal turns experience into evidence. Record the decision honestly, not just the profit or loss.

### The revenge cycle

1. A loss, missed trade, or premature exit creates frustration.
2. You feel urgency to repair the result.
3. Standards are lowered or size is increased.
4. A second emotional outcome creates more urgency.
5. Review is postponed because you want one more chance.

The cycle must be interrupted before the next order.

### Early warning signs

- scanning random stocks immediately after a stop
- thinking in terms of “getting it back”
- changing the risk percentage
- entering without screenshots or a written plan
- choosing a faster timeframe
- feeling unable to leave the desk
- hiding the trade from the journal

One warning may be manageable. Several together require a full stop.

### The Ronin reset protocol

After a trigger event:

1. Close the order ticket.
2. Stand away from the screen for at least ten minutes.
3. Record the event, emotion, and rule at risk.
4. Reclassify the market and current watchlist.
5. Allow another trade only if it was planned before the emotional event and still meets every rule.
6. If emotional intensity remains high, end live trading for the session.

A reset is not punishment. It protects the business from a temporary loss of decision quality.

### Consistency goals

Daily P&L is partly outside your control. Process goals are not. Examples:

- no entries above the LOD ATR limit
- every trade belongs to one parent setup
- every stop is structural
- no order without a pre-entry screenshot
- every emotional trigger is logged
- no trade after the drawdown threshold is reached

Consistency grows when success is measured by these behaviors.

## Real example or short trading story

A stop is hit, and within minutes you begin searching for another trade to recover the loss. Your standards drop because the real goal is no longer a valid setup; it is emotional relief.

You recognize the revenge cycle and activate the reset protocol: step away, record the emotion, review the rule violation risk, and return only when the next decision can stand on its own.

Consistency is protected one interruption at a time.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Recognize the revenge cycle early.** Urgency, size changes, and lower standards are warning signs.
2. **Run the reset protocol.** Step away, record the emotion, and review the rule at risk.
3. **Return only when the next trade can stand on its own.** It must not be a recovery attempt.
4. **Measure consistency by rule adherence, not by daily P&L.**

**Rule to remember**

> The next trade does not exist to repair the last result.

## Common mistakes

- Taking the next trade to recover the previous loss
- Increasing size after frustration
- Skipping the journal because you do not want to face the decision
