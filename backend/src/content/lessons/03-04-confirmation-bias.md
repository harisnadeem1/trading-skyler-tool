# Confirmation Bias and Story Attachment

## Why this matters

Relative strength is useful because it provides measurable evidence. It becomes dangerous when you use it selectively to defend a stock already chosen for emotional reasons.

## Clear explanation

Relative strength tells you how a stock is behaving compared with the market. It is not RSI. Use it to confirm leadership, never to replace price and structure.

### How bias enters the chart

Once you like a company, remember a previous winner, or hear a convincing thesis, the mind begins to filter information. A rising RS Rating receives attention, while a weakening RS line, damaged base, or poor market regime is explained away.

The system prevents this by placing evidence in order. Leadership is required, but leadership is not an entry. The daily chart still must form a Flat Base Breakout or Controlled Pullback, and demand still must return at a logical location.

### The disconfirmation checklist

Before adding a stock to the active list, write the evidence that would remove it:

- RS line loses its prior trend or diverges negatively
- stock stops holding up on weak market days
- group leadership deteriorates
- base becomes wide and loose
- pullback shows expanding sell volume
- price loses the key structural level
- the trigger becomes late or extended

This list must be written before the trade. After entry, the mind has a financial reason to reinterpret weakness.

### Story versus evidence

A strong catalyst can explain why institutions may care. It cannot repair poor price action. Use the story to create context, then demand proof from price, volume, and relative strength.

A useful sentence is:

> The story earns attention. The chart earns capital.

### The opposite-case exercise

For every active candidate, spend two minutes arguing why the trade should not be taken. The goal is not pessimism. It is to find the strongest contrary evidence before money is committed.

If the bearish or rejection case is stronger than the planned continuation case, the chart is not ready.

## Real example or short trading story

You like a company’s story and expect strong future growth. When the chart loses support, you call the drop temporary. When volume expands on the decline, you search for another bullish explanation.

The story is now controlling the chart.

You write the opposite case: what evidence would prove the leadership thesis is weakening? That question brings the decision back to observable behavior.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Write disconfirming evidence before entry.** Decide what would remove the stock from the list.
2. **Separate the story from the chart.** A catalyst earns attention; price, volume, RS, and structure earn capital.
3. **Run the opposite-case exercise.** Argue why the trade should not be taken.
4. **Respect the evidence after entry.** Do not rewrite invalidation because you like the company.

**Rule to remember**

> Evidence must be allowed to remove a stock from the list, even when the story remains attractive.

## Common mistakes

- Defending the company story after price loses structure
- Searching only for evidence that supports your opinion
- Moving the invalidation level after negative evidence appears

### Coach's note

Name the emotion that could make you bend **Confirmation Bias and Story Attachment**. Then return to the written rule before you act.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Choose ten historical trades that you strongly believed in. Hide the ticker and company name, then review only the price, volume, RS, and market context. Compare your blind classification with your original decision.

Record every case where the story changed your standard.

### Reflection

1. What does the stock’s price say that the RS line or rating cannot say by itself?
2. What evidence would prove that your leadership thesis is wrong?
3. How will you prevent a company story from overruling weakening price behavior?
