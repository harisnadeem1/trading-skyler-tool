# Build Weekly and Daily Watchlists

## Why this matters

A scanner finds candidates; a watchlist organizes decisions. Your watchlist should contain only the strongest charts with pre-marked levels and a clear reason for attention.

## Clear explanation

Your scanner gives you candidates, not trades. Your job is to reduce the list to genuine leaders with clean structure, enough liquidity, enough movement, and a real reason for institutions to care.

Scanners find candidates. Watchlists organize decisions.

### The weekly master list

Build the weekly list after the market closes on Friday or over the weekend. Start with momentum scans such as:

- six-month leaders
- three-month leaders
- one-month accelerators
- recent earnings gaps
- high-volume events
- stocks near new highs

Review the weekly chart first. Remove stocks with heavy overhead supply, declining major moving averages, or loose long-term structures.

### Classify every candidate

Each active candidate must be labeled as:

- **Flat Base Breakout**
- **Controlled Pullback**
- **Developing / Not Ready**

Do not create vague categories such as “interesting.” A classification tells you what behavior must occur next.

### Mark the plan

For every stock, record:

- catalyst
- group
- RS condition
- setup type
- key level
- pivot
- support area
- daily ATR
- earnings date
- what would make the chart actionable
- what would invalidate it before entry

### The daily focus list

The daily list should contain only the best candidates within practical distance of an entry. Five to ten names are often enough.

Prioritize by:

1. market alignment;
2. leadership quality;
3. structure cleanliness;
4. proximity to the trigger;
5. volume behavior;
6. entry efficiency.

### Use alerts

Set alerts near pivots and support areas. Alerts reduce screen time and prevent random chart hopping. When an alert fires, re-evaluate the entire setup rather than entering automatically.

### Promote and demote

A candidate is promoted when:

- the base tightens
- volume dries up
- the key level holds
- RS improves
- the market regime becomes more supportive

It is demoted when:

- price becomes loose
- support breaks
- earnings risk approaches unexpectedly
- RS deteriorates
- the group weakens
- entry becomes extended

### End-of-day maintenance

Remove broken setups. Update pivots. Save screenshots.

Add new leaders from fresh scans. A clean list improves execution because the decision was prepared before the market becomes emotional.

### How to apply it in real time

Scanners create raw candidates; watchlists create preparedness. A scanner can identify momentum, relative strength, earnings gaps, or proximity to highs, but it cannot determine whether the final third of a base is tight or whether a pullback is deteriorating. Human chart review converts the list into a small number of plans.

The weekend process should begin with weekly charts to eliminate poor long-term structures quickly. The daily chart then determines the setup stage: early watch, developing, near trigger, or invalid. During the week, the list is updated rather than rebuilt from nothing.

This preserves familiarity with the strongest names and prevents chasing whatever moved that morning.

Every active watchlist item should have a reason, a level, and a condition. “Strong stock” is not a plan. “Post-earnings leader forming a shallow flat base; watch 74.20 pivot if volume expands” is a plan.

## Real example or short trading story

On the weekend, you build a broad master list of leaders. Each evening, you reduce it to the names closest to a valid Flat Base Breakout or Controlled Pullback.

By the open, only a small focus list needs your attention. Levels, invalidation, catalyst dates, and alerts are already marked.

The trade feels calmer because most of the thinking happened before the pressure.

## Key rules / steps

1. Weekend: run one-, three-, and six-month momentum and catalyst scans.
2. Review weekly charts first and remove damaged structures or major overhead supply.
3. Classify daily candidates as Flat Base, Controlled Pullback, or No Setup.
4. Mark the pivot, support, invalidation area, earnings date, and key catalyst.
5. Prioritize five to ten names by proximity, quality, and market fit.
6. Set alerts and review only material changes during the session.

**Rule to remember**

> **The scanner produces names. Your chart review produces the edge.**

## Common mistakes

- Keeping too many mediocre names because the scanner returned them
- Keeping hundreds of symbols with no priority
- Adding a stock only after it breaks out
- Deleting a leader after one quiet day and losing context
- Writing vague notes that do not define what must happen next

### Coach's note

Watch for **focus and novelty bias**. Random movers and social-media excitement pull attention away from prepared leaders. A focused list reduces emotional decisions.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Build three lists: Leadership Universe, Developing Setups, and Actionable Soon. Limit the final list to ten symbols. For each actionable name, write one sentence containing setup, key level, confirmation, and disqualifier.

Remove any symbol for which that sentence cannot be written clearly.

### Reflection

1. Which candidate would you remove first, and what evidence makes it weaker?
2. How could FOMO or information noise pull you away from the prepared watchlist?
3. What facts must be recorded before a stock earns a place on your focus list?
