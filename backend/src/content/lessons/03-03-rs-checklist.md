# The Ronin RS Checklist

## Why this matters

Use the same short RS checklist every time so you do not call a familiar or popular stock a leader without evidence.

## Clear explanation

Relative strength tells you how a stock is behaving compared with the market. It is not RSI. Use it to confirm leadership, never to replace price and structure.

Relative strength should be evaluated at three stages: selection, setup development, and trigger.

### Stage 1: Selection

Ask:

- Is the RS rating in the top tier of the market?
- Is the RS line trending higher over several months?
- Is the stock outperforming its group?
- Is price near the upper part of its range?

The preferred candidate is already a leader before it reaches the active watchlist.

### Stage 2: Setup development

Inside a base or pullback, ask:

- Is the RS line holding near highs?
- Is it rising while price contracts?
- Does it avoid major breakdowns during market weakness?
- Is the stock maintaining its leadership rank?

A flat base with a collapsing RS line is less attractive because the stock is losing sponsorship during the exact period when it should be resisting supply.

### Stage 3: Trigger

At the breakout or reversal, ask:

- Is RS improving with price?
- Is the stock acting stronger than the index intraday?
- Does the breakout create an RS high?
- Is the group also participating?

### Scoring model

Use a simple 0–2 score for each category:

- long-term RS trend
- RS near highs
- RS during contraction
- intraday relative performance
- group leadership

A strong candidate scores consistently across categories. The score does not replace judgment; it prevents one attractive feature from dominating the decision.

### Ronin checklist

Before entry, I want to say:

> “This stock has led over time, held leadership during contraction, and is strengthening again at the trigger.”

If that sentence is not true, the setup deserves a lower priority.

### How to apply it in real time

A checklist prevents RS from becoming a vague compliment applied to any stock you like. The purpose is to rank leaders and reject candidates whose apparent strength is temporary, late, or unsupported by price structure.

The checklist should be applied at two moments. First, during watchlist construction, to decide whether the stock deserves continued attention. Second, immediately before entry, to confirm that leadership has not weakened during the setup.

A stock can enter the watchlist with elite RS and lose that character during a deep base.

No single answer is decisive. A slightly lower rating can be offset by rising short-term RS and a strong group. A high rating cannot offset collapsing RS during a breakdown.

## Real example or short trading story

A stock has an RS Rating of 97, but its current RS line is rolling over and the stock is losing support. Another has a slightly lower rating but is making a fresh RS high while its base tightens.

A single number cannot complete the decision.

You move through the checklist: price leadership, current RS behavior, market-relative action, group context, and confirmation at the trigger.

## Key rules / steps

1. RS rating is in the preferred leadership range, commonly 90 or higher for priority names.
2. RS line is rising, near highs, or making a constructive divergence.
3. Price is near recent highs and above relevant major averages.
4. The stock outperforms its industry peers or is a clear group leader.
5. Leadership persists through the base or pullback.
6. The RS behavior aligns with a real catalyst and sufficient liquidity.

**Rule to remember**

> **A strong story cannot repair weak relative strength.**

## Common mistakes

- Treating an RS number as a buy signal without reading the chart
- Turning the checklist into an automatic score that ignores chart context
- Keeping a stock because its twelve-month rating remains high after recent deterioration
- Comparing a stock only with SPY when its group is collapsing
- Allowing strong RS to justify an extended entry

### Coach's note

Watch for **confirmation bias**. Once you like a stock, the brain searches for evidence that agrees. RS must be used as a filter, not as permission to ignore deterioration.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Create a scorecard for twenty candidates with six binary fields from this lesson. Rank them, then manually inspect the top five and bottom five. Adjust the score only if a field repeatedly fails to distinguish true leaders; do not change it to make a favorite stock qualify.

### Reflection

1. What does the stock’s price say that the RS line or rating cannot say by itself?
2. What evidence would prove that your leadership thesis is wrong?
3. How will you prevent a company story from overruling weakening price behavior?
