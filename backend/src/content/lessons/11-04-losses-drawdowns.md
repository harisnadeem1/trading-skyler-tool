# Handling Losses, Losing Streaks, and Drawdowns

## Why this matters

Losses are expected. Drawdowns become dangerous when they change behavior: hesitation on valid setups, oversized recovery trades, wider stops, random strategy changes, or complete avoidance of review.

## Clear explanation

The chart defines invalidation. Your account rules determine position size. Never reverse that order to force a larger trade.

### The three drawdown questions

After a losing sequence, ask in order:

1. **Were the trades valid?** Review market, stock, setup, entry, risk, and management.
2. **Was the market regime classified correctly?** Several valid setups failing together may indicate hostile conditions.
3. **Did execution change?** Look for chasing, oversized positions, stop negotiation, or skipped confirmations.

Do not change the system until these questions are answered with evidence.

### Normal variance versus process failure

A sequence of planned losses inside valid setups can occur. That is variance. Repeated violations or continued aggression while leaders fail is process failure.

The response differs:

- normal variance requires consistency and sample-size awareness
- process failure requires reduced activity, review, and correction
- hostile market conditions require defensive exposure or no trade

### The Ronin drawdown protocol

Define thresholds before the drawdown begins. A practical protocol can include:

- after three consecutive losses: stop for the session and review
- after a defined weekly R drawdown: reduce new-trade risk and require A-grade setups
- after a larger account drawdown: pause live trading, use replay, and complete a formal review
- return to normal risk only after several rule-compliant executions, not one winning trade

The exact thresholds should be written in the personal risk plan. They must never be invented while frustrated.

### Fear after losses

Reducing size can be appropriate. Refusing every valid setup because the last one failed is different. Use smaller size and the same rules to rebuild execution confidence.

Do not create extra confirmation requirements that never existed in the tested method.

## Real example or short trading story

You take several valid trades and the market produces a normal losing streak. The temptation is to increase risk, change setups, or trade more often to recover faster.

You first ask three questions: Is this normal variance? Did execution weaken? Did the market regime change?

You reduce pressure, review the evidence, and follow the drawdown protocol. Recovery begins with better decisions, not larger bets.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Ask the three drawdown questions.** Is this normal variance, an execution problem, or a regime change?
2. **Reduce pressure before changing the system.** Lower activity or exposure when the protocol requires it.
3. **Review a real sample.** Separate rule violations from valid losses.
4. **Resume normal aggression only after evidence and execution quality recover.**

**Rule to remember**

> A drawdown is managed by reducing emotional freedom and increasing process control.

## Common mistakes

- Increasing risk to recover faster
- Changing the system after a normal losing streak
- Continuing at normal aggression when execution or market conditions have deteriorated
