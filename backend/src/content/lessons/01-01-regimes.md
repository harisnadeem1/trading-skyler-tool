# The Four Ronin Market Regimes

## Why this matters

The same setup behaves differently in a strong market and a hostile market. The regime tells you how selective or active to be.

## Clear explanation

Before you judge any stock, decide what the market is rewarding. The same setup can work in one regime and fail quickly in another.

The same stock setup can behave very differently in different market environments. The purpose of a regime model is not to predict the next index move. It is to decide how much opportunity deserves your attention today.

The Ronin framework uses four practical states.

### Aggressive

Conditions are strongly supportive of long swing trades.

Typical evidence:

- major indexes are above rising short- and intermediate-term moving averages
- leaders are making new highs
- breakouts hold their pivots and produce follow-through
- controlled pullbacks are bought quickly
- breadth is healthy enough to support multiple groups
- existing positions are working and requiring little defense

In an Aggressive environment, the focus is on the best leaders and cleanest continuation structures. Aggressive does not mean careless. It means the market is rewarding the system’s behavior.

### Constructive

The market remains tradable, but selectivity is required.

Evidence may include:

- indexes are mixed but not technically damaged
- leadership is concentrated in a few groups
- some breakouts work while others fail
- pullbacks require more confirmation
- volatility is higher than normal
- watchlist quality is good but not broad

In a Constructive environment, fewer trades may be appropriate. The strongest stocks with the cleanest structures receive priority.

### Defensive

The market is punishing risk or becoming unstable.

Common signs:

- repeated failed breakouts
- leaders losing the 8, 21, or 50-day averages
- wide index reversals
- heavy distribution
- narrowing breadth
- existing positions failing quickly
- strong stocks no longer separating from the market

In a Defensive state, new exposure should be limited and confirmation standards should rise. Cash is useful because it protects both capital and decision quality.

### No Trade

The environment offers no clear advantage for the system.

Examples:

- severe market breakdown
- extreme headline volatility
- repeated gap reversals
- very few liquid leaders
- no valid setups on the watchlist
- your own execution is emotionally compromised

No Trade is not a forecast that prices must fall. It is a decision that the current environment does not justify risk.

### Regime changes are gradual

Do not switch from fully aggressive to completely defensive because of one red day. Look for a cluster of evidence:

- index structure
- leadership behavior
- breakout follow-through
- breadth
- your portfolio feedback

The regime is a working label, not an absolute truth. Update it as evidence changes.

### Daily regime statement

Before each session, write one sentence:

> “The market is currently Constructive because leadership is narrow, indexes remain above the 50-day average, and recent breakouts are producing mixed follow-through.”

That sentence forces you to connect your label to observable evidence.

### How to apply it in real time

A regime label is useful only if it changes behavior. The Ronin model uses four states because market conditions are not simply “good” or “bad.” Aggressive conditions support normal participation in the best setups. Constructive conditions support selective participation with confirmation.

Defensive conditions demand smaller exposure, fewer trades, and faster respect for failure. No Trade conditions make capital preservation the primary task.

The classification should reflect evidence from indexes and leaders. Index position relative to key moving averages matters, but the behavior of the leading stock universe often gives earlier information. If indexes appear stable while breakouts fail and leaders lose support, the environment is weaker than the index chart suggests.

Conversely, when leaders hold firm during an index pullback, the next constructive phase may be developing.

Regimes are dynamic. The purpose is not to make a permanent forecast; it is to choose the correct operating mode for the next session and update it when evidence changes.

## Real example or short trading story

The indexes are still above the 50-day average, so the market looks acceptable at first glance. But your best leaders are failing breakouts, losing the 21 EMA, and closing weakly.

You could keep calling the market Constructive because the index has not broken. Instead, you let leader behavior update the evidence and move toward Defensive.

The label is useful only because it changes how active and selective you are.

## Key rules / steps

1. Aggressive: indexes trend constructively, breadth improves, leaders break out and hold, and existing positions produce follow-through.
2. Constructive: conditions are usable but mixed; take only the cleanest setups and demand confirmation.
3. Defensive: repeated failures, distribution, weak breadth, or damaged leaders; reduce activity and protect open gains.
4. No Trade: disorder is dominant and there is no reliable long-side follow-through; wait for repair.

**Rule to remember**

> **Market condition changes how aggressively you act; it does not change the definition of a valid setup.**

## Common mistakes

- Using one index candle to declare the whole market healthy or broken
- Calling the market Aggressive because one index closed green
- Keeping an Aggressive label after several days of failed leadership
- Using the regime as a prediction rather than a risk posture
- Taking marginal setups because the regime is favorable

### Coach's note

Watch for **patience and activity bias**. A quiet market can create the false feeling that you are falling behind. Activity is not productivity; the regime decides how much opportunity exists.

## Practice / Reflection question

### Practice

Review the previous sixty sessions. Mark each transition between the four regimes using only information available at that time. Under every transition, list three pieces of evidence from indexes and three from leaders.

The exercise is successful when the label explains behavior, not when it identifies the exact market bottom or top.

### Reflection

1. What market evidence would make you change your regime classification?
2. How would impatience or the urge to trade distort this decision?
3. What must your leader basket show before you become more aggressive?
