# Hesitation, Impulse, and Trigger Discipline

## Why this matters

Execution pressure is highest near the trigger. Before the move, fear can create hesitation. After the move, urgency can create an impulsive chase.

The solution is a rehearsed decision made before the candle accelerates.

## Clear explanation

A 30-minute trigger is valid only after the higher-timeframe setup is complete. Speed does not repair weak structure.

### Three execution states

**Prepared:** The setup, trigger, stop, size, and invalidation are defined before the level trades.

**Hesitant:** The setup qualifies, but you delay because of recent losses, fear of being wrong, or a need for extra confirmation not included in the plan.

**Impulsive:** The planned trigger is gone, but you enter late because price movement creates urgency.

Only the prepared state supports consistent execution.

### The pre-entry script

Before the alert fires, write:

- parent setup
- trigger type
- exact confirmation required
- maximum acceptable entry
- structural stop
- Low-of-Day ATR limit
- reason to cancel the order
- position size

When the level trades, compare reality with the script. Do not invent a new rule in the moment.

### Handling hesitation

If the setup qualifies but fear prevents action, do not chase later. Record the missed execution and study the cause after the session. Common causes include:

- recent losing streak
- position size too large for emotional tolerance
- unclear invalidation
- insufficient preparation
- lack of trust from inadequate historical study

The correction depends on the cause. More screen time is not always the answer.

### Handling impulse

If price moves beyond the maximum entry, the decision changes from “take the trigger” to “wait for new structure.” The stock may continue. That does not reactivate the old plan.

## Real example or short trading story

Your trigger appears. One part of you hesitates because the last trade lost; another part wants to buy immediately before the calculation is finished.

You use the pre-entry script: setup, trigger, stop, Low-of-Day ATR, position size, and market condition.

The script creates a small pause between emotion and action. That pause is where discipline lives.

## Key rules / steps

1. **Know the three states.** Before trigger, at trigger, and after trigger require different actions.
2. **Run the pre-entry script.** Confirm setup, trigger, stop, Low-of-Day ATR, position size, and market condition.
3. **Handle hesitation with the written plan.** Do not let the previous loss veto a qualified trade.
4. **Handle impulse with the same script.** Do not click before the checks are complete.

**Rule to remember**

> Prepare before the trigger, act only inside the plan, and let the old decision expire when the entry becomes late.

## Common mistakes

- Clicking before the pre-entry checks are complete
- Freezing because the previous trade lost
- Using speed as an excuse to skip the written plan

Use replay or simulation for twenty valid 30-minute triggers. Before each, complete the pre-entry script. Track:

- whether you acted at the planned location
- whether you hesitated
- whether you chased
- what emotion appeared
- whether the position size affected behavior
